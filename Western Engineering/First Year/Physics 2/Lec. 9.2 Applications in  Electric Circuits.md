# Applications: Capacitive Touchscreens

![[Pasted image 20240127154811.png]]

- A capacitive touchscreen is made of a single capacitive layer that is also coated with a conductive material and glass. The finger or other conductive materials that come in touch forms a second capacitive layer. 
- [[Voltage]] is applied through the layer and that creates a circuit with a single capacitor. When a conductive object is touched, it acts as a second capacitor parallel to the existing capacitor. 
- The total [[Capacitance]] increases, the [[Voltage]] drops, the frequency of the electromagnetic oscillation changes.

- In many modern capacitive touchscreens, the position of a touch is detected using an array of [[Capacitors]]. 
- These capacitors are designed so that the [[Electric Field Lines]] are not entirely contained within the capacitor, instead spreading out to just beyond the surface of the screen.
- When a conducting material, such as your finger, moves close to the screen, it becomes part of the capacitor, changing the capacitance. 
- As the change in the capacitance depends on the distance between the capacitor and the finger, the change in [[Capacitance]] of several nearby [[Capacitors]] allows the coordinates of the finger to be found.

# Application: Condenser Microphone

- Condenser = capacitor 
- One plate of the capacitor is a flexible diaphragm 
- Charge on plates is held constant. 
- Sound waves vibrate the diaphragm, changing the spacing between the capacitor plates. 
- This changes C, and so changes V
