
[[Lec. 2.1 Coulomb's Law]]: The [[Electric Force]] exerted by a charged particle $q_1$ on a second charge $q_2$ is given by:
$$F_{12} = k \frac{q_1q_2}{r^2} r$$
	r = distance between two charges
	k = Coulomb Constant → $8.998*10^9 Nm^2/C^2$
	r = Unit vector pointing from charge 1 to charge 2
	
[[Electric Force]]: 
	- Force is a vector quantity. The [[Electric Force]] is directed along the line joining the two particles. $F_12$ is:
		- positive (repulsive) when the two charges have the same sign.
		- negative (attractive) between charges of opposite sign
- The magnitude of the force depends only on the magnitudes of the charges and the separation.

$$F_{12} = k \frac{q_1q_2}{r^2} r$$
	-Obeys Newton's Third Law $F_12 = -F_21$
	-Like charges repel $F_12 = k \frac{q_1q_2}{r^2} r > 0$
	-Unlike charges attract  $k \frac{q_1q_2}{r^2} r$ < 0

	For historical reasons, *k* is sometimes expressed in terms of the "permittivity of free space"

$$\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}$$
	
$\epsilon_0$ = Permittivity of free space → $8.854*10^-12 C^2/Nm^2$ 
	[[Lec. 2.1 Coulomb's Law]] the takes the form: 

$$F_{12} = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0} \frac{q_1q_2}{r^2} r$$
**More than two charges...**
- If we have multiple charges, each charge exerts a force on each other charge. The total force on a charge is the vector sum of all the forces due to the other charges
- This is called the principle of [[Principle of Linear Superposition]]

In general, the force on the a charge due to a collection of other charges is just the sum of the forces due to all the other charges.

$$F_{tot} = F_1 + F_2 + F_3 + ...$$
$$=\sum F_i$$
*i* = all other charges
