
According to [[Lec. 2.1 Coulomb's Law]], the force that one charge exerts on another depends on the distance between them. But:
	-How does nature know what force should be exerted?
	- How do the charges know how far apart they are?
	- How does one charge know how big the other charge is?
	
- *These questions motivate the concept of the electric field*

Suppose there is an [[Electric Force]] *F* acting on a charge $q_0$. It doesn't matter what causes the force.

We define the electric field at the location of our charge $q_0$ to be: 

$$E = \frac{F}{q_0}$$
Technically, we need to take a limit on *F* as $q_0$ goes to zero, so the charge does not change the field itself. Normally this is not something we need to worry about.

- The electric field is the [[Electric Force]] per unit charge $E=\frac{F}{q_0}$

- This is analogous to the gravitational field, which is the gravitational force per unit mass. → $g = \frac{F_g}{m}$

-----------------------------------------------
-The electric field exists at every point in space
-A charge (or distribution of charges) produces an electric field.
-The electric field then exerts a force on other charges

**The Electric Field of a Point Charge** 

- Suppose we have a point charge *q*. We know that the force this charge would exert on a *small* test charge $q_0$ located a distance *r* away is 

$$F = k \frac{q_0q}{r^2} r$$
 - The electric field due to the pint charge *q* is then: 
$$F_12 = k \frac{q_1q_2}{r^2} r$$
- The electric field due to a point charge *q* is:

$$E = k \frac{q}{r^2}r$$
___________________________________________________
- The electric field is a *vector*: it has a magnitude and a direction
- *r* is a unit vector pointing from the charge to the point charge.
- if *q* > 0 the field points away from the charge.
- if *q* < 0 the field points inwards, towards the charge.

 - Given the electric field, it is easy to calculate the [[Electric Force]] acting on a charge $q_0$:

$$E = \frac{F}{q_0} → F=q_0E$$![[Principle of Superposition]]
