> [!note] Theorem 2
> A language is [[Context-free Language|context-free]] iff there exists some [[Pushdown Automaton|PDA]] that recognizes it.

The proof follows the same two-direction strategy as DFA/NFA equivalence:
- For every [[Context-free Grammar|CFG]] generating L, some PDA recognizes L (Sipser, Lemma 2.21)
- For every PDA recognizing L, some CFG generates L (Sipser, Lemma 2.27)

**Consequences**
- "Context-free" now has two interchangeable faces, exactly as "regular" does: generated by some CFG ⟺ recognized by some PDA. This is the CFL analogue of [[Kleene's Theorem]] — the second Kleene-style theorem of the course
- To show a language context-free, use whichever face is easier: a grammar for {0ⁿ1ⁿ}, a machine for balanced parentheses
- One-line corollary: an NFA is just a PDA that never touches its stack, so every regular language is PDA-recognizable — REG ⊆ CFL again, now proved on the machine side ([[Regular Languages are Context-free|Lecture 8]] proved it on the grammar side)

> [!warning] "Needs a PDA" carries two obligations
> Claiming a language needs a PDA means giving **a PDA (or CFG) that works** *and* **a proof that no DFA does** (e.g. the [[Pumping Lemma]]). One-sided evidence is half an answer.
