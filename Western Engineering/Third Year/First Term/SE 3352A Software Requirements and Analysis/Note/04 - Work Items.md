### Reading — Example User Stories (Mike Cohn)

Three real starting backlogs, written as one-line stories in the [[User Story]] template:

| Product | Story groups |
|---|---|
| Scrum Alliance website (2004) | Profiles · News · Courses and Events · FAQs · Resources · Jobs · Articles · Home Page · Ratings · What is Scrum? · Registry · Membership · For Trainers Only |
| Video training site | Course Catalog · Account Management · Course Creation · Buying Courses · License Admin · Viewing Courses · Course Completion · Forums · Announcements · Quizzes · Comments · Course Evaluation · Reporting · Affiliates · Non-Functional |
| PlanningPoker.com | Game · Archive · Account · Non-Functional |

What to take from it:
- **The groups act like [[Epic|epics]]/[[Feature|features]]** — stories are organized by area of the product, the same hierarchy as [[Work Items]].
- **Not every story is good.** The author says so up front; many would fail parts of [[INVEST]] (some are too big, some lack a "so that" reason).
- **Bracketed notes and open questions** ("Note: this could just be an email link", "After each survey? After a set period?") show the **Negotiable** idea — the story is a placeholder for a conversation.
- **Non-functional requirements can be stories too**, e.g. a user wanting fast responses or Unicode support, or a developer wanting the site to meet accessibility guidelines — compare [[Non-Functional Requirement]].
- **Roles go beyond "user"**: site visitor, member, editor, admin, trainer, moderator, even a search engine or a researcher.
