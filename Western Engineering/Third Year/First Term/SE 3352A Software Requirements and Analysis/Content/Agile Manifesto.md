The **Agile Manifesto** was created in 2001 by 17 experienced developers (including Scrum's founders, Ken Schwaber and Jeff Sutherland). It set the core values of agile software development as four value pairs — in each, the left value is prioritized over the right:

- Individuals and interactions **over** processes and tools
- Working software **over** comprehensive documentation
- Customer collaboration **over** contract negotiation
- Responding to change **over** following a plan

*(agilemanifesto.org)*

**The twelve Agile Principles** (Agile Alliance) back up the Manifesto:

1. Satisfy the customer through early, continuous delivery of valuable software
2. Welcome changing requirements, even late in development
3. Deliver working software frequently (weeks, not months)
4. Business people and developers collaborate daily
5. Build around motivated individuals — give them support and trust
6. Face-to-face conversation is the most effective communication
7. Working software is the primary measure of progress
8. Maintain a sustainable, constant pace indefinitely
9. Continuous attention to technical excellence and good design
10. Simplicity — maximize the amount of work *not* done
11. Best architectures/requirements/designs emerge from self-organizing teams
12. Regularly reflect and adjust behavior to become more effective
