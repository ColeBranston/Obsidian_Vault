**Elicitation** is a discovery process that brings forward information relevant to the project by drawing it out from stakeholders and other sources.

It aims to identify the cause of the business problem (or the reason to pursue an opportunity), and to gather the information needed to derive requirements sufficient for a solution to be developed and implemented.

## Success factors

| Factor | What it means |
|---|---|
| Preparation | Decide how to conduct sessions, which stakeholders to involve, and in what order — before elicitation starts |
| Active stakeholder engagement | Collect input from a broad, diverse set of stakeholders; record and act on it so they don't feel ignored |
| Defined business/organizational need | Understanding the need, problem, or opportunity drives which information, stakeholders, and techniques to use |
| Domain knowledge | The person eliciting should know the domain (or have access to subject matter experts) to ask the right questions |
| Plan for the elicitation | Pick a technique per project/requirement type, schedule sessions, decide the deliverable, and plan how to record and communicate results |

Finally: **conduct** the elicitation activity, then **document and communicate** the results.
