**FDM** gives each user their **own frequency band, all of the time** — the link is cut lengthwise.

Where you meet it: radio and television broadcast · cable · early telephone trunks.

Like [[Time-Division Multiplexing]], it **reserves capacity in advance**, which is what makes it [[Circuit Switching]]. A silent user's band stays theirs and nobody else may use it.

**Cut lengthwise — a band each, always.** A user owns a *row*:

| freq ↑ | t1 | t2 | t3 | t4 | t5 | t6 | t7 | t8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **band 1** | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| **band 2** | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 |
| **band 3** | 3 | 3 | 3 | 3 | — | — | — | — |
| **band 4** | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 |

*User 3 goes silent and their space stays theirs — the dashes are capacity nobody can lend out. Compare [[Time-Division Multiplexing]], which cuts the same grid the other way.*
