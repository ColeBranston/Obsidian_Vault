**Wired** media confine the signal to something somebody installed; **wireless** media radiate it, and the air is shared.

| Medium | Typical rate | Delay | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|
| Twisted pair | 1–10 Gb/s | few µs/km | Two insulated copper wires twisted so the twist cancels interference · Ethernet in every building |
| Coaxial cable | 100s of Mb/s | few µs/km | One copper core inside a braided shield · cable TV and cable Internet |
| Optical fiber | 10s–100s Gb/s | ~5 µs/km | Light bounced along a glass core · backbones, undersea, now to the home |
| Wi-Fi (WLAN) | 10s–100s Mb/s | well under 1 ms | Radio access point · tens of meters, shared with everyone in the building |
| Cellular 4G/5G | 10s of Mb/s+ | tens of ms | A mast covering a cell · kilometers of reach, survives you moving |
| Satellite | up to ~100 Mb/s | up to 270 ms round trip | A relay in orbit · a geostationary hop is 36,000 km each way, and that distance *is* the delay |

Signal speed in fiber is about $2 \times 10^8$ m/s — the number used for [[Propagation Delay]].
