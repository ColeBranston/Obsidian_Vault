
**A piece of reasoning**.

Ex. "When choosing a major in university, you should choose the major that interests you most. This is because you will always do best at what you find most interesting, and you should choose the major that you will do best in."

**Premise 1:** You will always do best at what you find most interesting.
**Premise 2:** You should choose the major that you will do best in.
**Conclusion:** When choosing a major in university, you should choose the major that interest you most.

### What Is Critical Thinking

- “Critical thinking is the ability to think clearly and rationally about what to do or what to believe.” (Lau, C1) 
- (In the previous example, we thought clearly and rationally about proposed reasons for choosing majors in a certain way.

**Note:**

1. critical thinking is an ability or a skill or ability like riding a bike, rather an a body of knowledge.
2. Critical thinking applies broadly, both to theoretical reasoning (what to believe) and practical reasoning (what to do).

#### Involves...

- Communication skills (understanding others, expressing oneself effectively)
- Information-gathering skills (gathering relevant information, assessing the credibility of sources)
- Argumentation skills (identifying, understanding, and analyzing arguments, producing good arguments)

#### What is Critical Thinking For?

**Critical Thinking is a domain-general skill**

- The ability to think critically is useful no matter what you do.
- It is useful for any career.
- Ex. Choosing a course of action to maximize profits in a company, assessing policy decision, resolving conflicts with colleagues.
- It is useful for everyday life.
- Ex. assessing claims on the internet, conversing with friends. 

#### Critical thinking is not the same as being critical

- Critical thinking need not involve being argumentative, judgmental, or overly critical.
- Critical thinking can be used to better help and cooperate with others.

#### How to improve critical thinking skills

- Critical thinking is a skill or ability.
	- It's something you *know how* to do, rather than a body of knowledge that you know
	- Like playing the piano, riding a bike
- Acquiring an ability usually involves...
	- A little bit of explicit instruction
	- A whole lot of practice
