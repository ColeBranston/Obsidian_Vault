An argument is **sound** when it is valid *and* all its premises are true.

- A valid argument whose premises are false is of little use: it shows that the conclusion *would* be true if the premises *were* true, but if the premises are not true this is not very helpful.
- This is why what we normally care about is the *soundness* of an argument, which requires both that the it be valid *and* that its premises be true.

- When an argument is not sound, it is **unsound**

**Must a sound argument have a true conclusion?** -> **YES**
