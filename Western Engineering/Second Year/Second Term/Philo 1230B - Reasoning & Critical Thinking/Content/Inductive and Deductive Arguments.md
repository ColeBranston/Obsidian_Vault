**Inductively Strong Argument Valid Argument**

- Being inductively strong or valid is a matter of the strength of the connection between an argument's premises and conclusion.

**Inductive Argument Deductive Argument**

- Being inductive or deductive is a matter of what an argument is "trying" to do.

>[!note] Deductive Arguments
>- A **deductive argument** is an argument that is intended to be valid.
>
>If Freddie is a fish, then Freddie can't fly. Freddie is a fish. It follows for sure that Freddie can't fly.

>[!note] Inductive and Deductive Arguments
>An **inductive argument** is an argument that is intended to be inductively strong.
>
>Halloween has been rainy for the past five years in a row. It will be a rainy this year, too!

**Note:**

- Sometimes it is not clear whether a given argument is deductive or inductive because it is not clear whether it is intended to be valid or inductively strong.

-> Halloween has been rainy for the past five years in a row. For sure it will be a rainy this year too!
