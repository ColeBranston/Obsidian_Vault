
- **Critical Thinking**: A metacognitive skill (thinking about thinking) that requires awareness of reasoning principles, reflection, and efforts to avoid biases and maintain objectivity. Mastery involves **theory**, **practice**, and **attitude**.

#### 1. **Theory**

- Knowledge of reasoning principles: **laws of logic**, **scientific methods**.
- Awareness of mistakes: **logical fallacies**, **cognitive biases**.

#### 2. **Practice**

- Theory must be applied through exercises, debates, and reflection.
- Learning is reinforced by connecting ideas and consistent application.

#### 3. **Attitude**

- Requires motivation and a willingness to challenge habits.
    
- Common obstacles:
    
    - Preferring easy answers.
    - Relying on gut feelings.
    - Avoiding criticism or reflection.

- Key dispositions for improvement:
    
    1. **Truth-seeking**: Striving to understand reality.
    2. **Open-mindedness**: Considering new ideas fairly.
    3. **Analyticity**: Evaluating reasons and evidence.
    4. **Systematicity**: Approaching problems methodically.
    5. **Confidence in reasoning**: Trusting and evaluating one’s judgment.
    6. **Inquisitiveness**: Eagerness to learn.
    7. **Maturity of judgment**: Avoiding hasty conclusions and considering multiple perspectives.

- **Cognitive Biases**: Be alert to biases like overconfidence and confirmation bias, which hinder reasoning. Recognizing these is critical to improving thought processes.
