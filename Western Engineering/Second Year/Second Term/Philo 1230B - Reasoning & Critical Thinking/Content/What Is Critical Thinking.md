**Critical thinking is the ability to think clearly and rationally about what to do or what to believe**. 

It includes the ability to engage in reflective and independent thinking. Someone with critical thinking skills is able to do the following: 

- Understand the logical connections between ideas
- Identify, construct and evaluate arguments
- Detect inconsistencies and common mistakes in reasoning
- Solve problems systematically
- Identify the relevance and importance in ideas
- Reflect on the justification of one's own beliefs and values

Critical thinking isn't a matter of accumulating information -> knowing a lot of facts doesn't make you a critical thinker. A critical thinker is that which deduces consequences from what he knows, and knows how to use information to solve problems, and to seek relevant sources of information to inform himself. 

Critical thinking isn't being argumentative or being critical of other people. Although used in exposing fallacies and bad reasoning, critical thinking can play an important role in cooperative reasoning and constructive tasks. 

Critical thinking can help us...

 1. acquire knowledge
 2. improve our theories
 3. strength arguments

Can be used to enhance work process and improve social institutions.

Critical thinking is compatible with thinking "out-of-the-box", challenging consensus and pursuing less popular approaches. Likely, critical thinking is essential to creativity because we need critical thinking to evaluate and improve our creative ideas. 

### The Importance to Critical Thinking

1. is a **Domain-general** thinking skill.

the ability to think clearly and rationally is important in everything -> Not restricted to a particular subject matter.

2. very important in the **new knowledge economy**

the economy is driven by information and technology and changes rapidly requiring you to be able to analyze diverse sources of information and integrate into solutions.

3. **Enhances language** and **presentation skills**

improves the way we express ideas -> learning to analyze the logical structure of texts, furthers comprehensive ability

4. **Promotes Creativity**

creative solutions derive from not just new ideas, but requires critical thinking to evaluate these new ideas selecting the best ones and modifying them if necessary.

5. Crucial for **self-reflection**

for a meaningful life and to structure our lives accordingly, we need to justify and reflect on our values and decisions. Critical thinking provides the tools for this process of self-evaluation. 

6. **Foundation** of **Science** and **Democracy**

science requires critical use of reason in experimentation and theory confirmation. Ex. Current democracy requires its citizens to critical think about social issues and to inform their judgements about proper governance and to overcome biases and prejudice. 

## Defining Critical Thinking

Critical thinking emphasizes **clarity**, **rationality**, and a systematic approach to evaluating beliefs and decisions. Below are notable definitions in chronological order:

---

#### **John Dewey (1910)**

- Introduced the concept of **reflective thinking**, focusing on:
    - Actively, persistently, and carefully considering beliefs or knowledge based on supporting grounds and conclusions.
    - Emphasized **suspended judgment**: Inquiry is necessary to understand a problem before attempting solutions.
    - Critical thinking transforms inferences into **tested proofs**.

---

#### **Watson-Glaser (1980)**

- Defined critical thinking as a **composite of attitudes, knowledge, and skills**:
    1. **Attitudes of inquiry**: Recognizing problems and the need for evidence.
    2. **Knowledge**: Understanding valid inferences and evidence evaluation.
    3. **Skills**: Applying attitudes and knowledge logically.

---

#### **Robert Ennis (1987)**

- Critical thinking is **reasonable reflective thinking** aimed at deciding what to believe or do.

---

#### **Scriven & Paul (1987)**

- Critical thinking involves **conceptualizing, applying, analyzing, synthesizing, and evaluating information** using:
    - Universal intellectual values: **clarity, accuracy, relevance, depth, and fairness**.
    - Examining elements of thought: Purpose, assumptions, evidence, conclusions, alternative viewpoints, and implications.

---

#### **Peter A. Facione (1990)**

- Critical thinking is **purposeful, self-regulatory judgment** involving:
    - Skills: **Interpretation, analysis, evaluation, inference, and explanation**.
    - Dispositions: **Inquisitiveness, open-mindedness, honesty, fairness, diligence, and persistence**.
    - Aims to develop critical thinkers who are informed, rational, and contribute to a democratic society.

---

These definitions highlight critical thinking as both a **skill set** and a **disposition**, essential for personal growth, education, and civic life.
