### Co-premises VS. Independent Premises

- Argument maps allow us to depict the difference between two ways in which premises can support a conclusions:
	1. The premises work together (co-premises_
	2. The premises work independently (independent premises)

### Conveying information about co premises vs independent premises

- Simply putting these arguments in standard form does not depict whether their premises are co-premises or independent premises. 
- Argument maps provide a nice way of distinguishing between the two kinds of cases.

### One strategy for multi-layered argument mapping

1. Identify the conclusion (i.e., the main conclusion). 
2. Identify the main argument. 
3. Identify any sub-arguments

>[!example] 
>	People shouldn’t remove weeds from their lawns. The reason for this is that it’s good for pollinators. There are two reasons for this: First, they provide them food. Second, they provide shelter.

Main argument (sub-argument 1): 
	(P1) Not removing weeds from one’s lawn is good for pollinators. 
	(C) People shouldn’t remove weeds from their lawns. 

Sub-argument 2: 
	(P2) Weeds provide food for pollinators.
	(P3) Weeds provide shelter for pollinators. 
	(P1) Not removing weeds from one’s lawn is good for pollinators.
