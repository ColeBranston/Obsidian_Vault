### Arguments Give Reason for Belief

- The purpose of arguments is to provide reason for accepting a conclusion.
- Understanding arguments is important because it helps us to evaluate whether there is in fact reason to believe a conclusion.

**Additionally arguments are composed of statements**

>[!Statements] 
>![[Statements]]

## Test For Being A Statement

- If you are not sure whether an expression $\phi$ is a statement, try appending "It is true that" to it.
- If "It is true that $\phi$" is grammatical, then $\phi$ is a statement. If it is not grammatical, $\phi$ is not a statement.

## Identifying Arguments

- But there are words and phrases that suggest that a statement is a conclusion or a premise.
- Examples:
	- The grass is healthy. *Therefore*, it rained this past week.
	- Winter is coming. *After all*, its' snowing.

- **Premise Indicators** are expressions that indicate a premise.
- **Conclusion Indicators** are expressions that indicate a conclusion.

# Putting Arguments in Standard Form

>[!Standard Form]
>![[Standard Form]]

### How to Put an Argument in Standard Form

- Number and display each premise on a separate line.
- Display the conclusion on the last line.
- Display the conclusion on the last line.
- Remove all premise indicators.
- Remove all conclusion indicators or replace with a simple conclusion indicator like "therefore".
- Rewrite premises and conclusion so that they are complete, explicit, and clear.

![[Pasted image 20250116013932.png]]

### Further Notes

- The conclusion of an argument might not be the last line in a text containing the argument.
- Sometimes the conclusion might not be explicitly stated.
	- Example: This cereal is full of toxic chemicals. How can you not believe it's OK to feed this to children?
- Similarly, a premise might not be explicitly stated. 

# Introduction to Validity and Soundness

>[!Validity]
>![[Validity]]

- The notion of validity is supposed to capture the intuitive idea of an argument's conclusion following from its premises.
- Valid arguments have conclusions that follow from their premises.

Example:

**(P1)** Joseph is tall. 
**(P2)** Manuela is taller than Joseph. 
**(C)** Therefore, Manuela is tall.
