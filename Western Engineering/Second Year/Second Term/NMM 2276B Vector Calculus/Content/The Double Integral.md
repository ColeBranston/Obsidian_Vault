- **2D Definite Integral**

-> **We use double integrals to calculate the volume under the region ($R$) NOT the area of the region itself**

- This means that we are integrating some function of 2 variables  (eg $f(x,y)$) over some region $R$ in the $(x,y)$ plane. As we've noted before and will continue to note - there's nothing special or sacred about $(x,y)$. These could be integrals over $(z,t)$ or $(y,z)$ or $(\alpha, \beta)$. The point is that we're integrating some function of two variables over a region in that 2-variable space.

$$
V = \iint_R f(x, y) \, dA
$$

$$
= \iint_R f(x, y) \, dx \, dy
$$
The quantity dA is the area element within R, over which we are evaluating f(x,y) for a contribution to the integral. dA = dxdy in Cartesian coordinates. In other coordinate systems, it can be a little more complicated (not a lot, but a little). We’ll see that when we get into polar coordinate integration (as an example).

The standard geometric interpretation of the integral in Eq. 2 is that it represents the volume between the xy plane and the function f(x,y) (see Figure 1)
