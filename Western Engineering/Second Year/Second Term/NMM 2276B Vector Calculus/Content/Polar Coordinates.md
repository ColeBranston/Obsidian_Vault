- We've seen polar coordinates just in different contexts. For our purpose, here is a general notation used to represent polar coordinate system.

**We represent the location of a point in $R^2$ as $(r,\theta)$.**

![[Pasted image 20250131103726.png]]

You may have used or seen different symbols or characters for the polar variables. Sometimes you use ρ instead of r. Sometimes you’ll see ϕ instead of θ. The use of (r, θ) is in my view probably the most common and that’s what we’ll use in this course.

- In Figure 1, the equations to get $(x,y)$ from $(r,\theta)$ are given:
$$
x = r \cdot \cos \theta \tag{1}
$$
$$
y = r \cdot \sin \theta \tag{2}
$$

- In order to do the opposite (get ($r,\theta$) from $(x,y)$), we have:
$$
r = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2} \tag{3}
$$
$$
\theta = \tan^{-1} \left( \frac{y}{x} \right) \tag{4}
$$
