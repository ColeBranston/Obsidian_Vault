A tensor is the mathematical representation of a physical entity that may be characterized by magnitude and multiple directions. 

Very unlikely to see in real application.

Example: Moment of Inertia

	When we introduced the moment of inertia, we always had to be very clear in our minds about what axis through the object we were calculating the moment of inertia. But if we want to generally define the moment of inertia for an object, we actually need to represent that it has “some moment of inertia about this axis, some different moment of inertia about this other axis, and maybe a third different moment of inertia about a third axis”- that sort of thing. So moment of inertia is a physical entity characterized by multiple “directions” (or axes).
