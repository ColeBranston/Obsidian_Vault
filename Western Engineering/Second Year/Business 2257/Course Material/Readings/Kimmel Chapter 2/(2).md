
## Understanding Retained Earnings

###### Definition

- Retained earnings are the cumulative amounts of net income that a company has retained over time.
    
- Changes in retained earnings are detailed in the statement of changes in equity.
    

###### Reporting

- Example: CGI Group Inc. reports retained earnings alongside share capital, contributed surplus, and accumulated other comprehensive income.

# Solvency Analysis

##### Importance of Solvency

- Investors and long-term lenders focus on a company's long-term solvency.
    
- Solvency indicates the ability to pay interest and repay debt at maturity.
    

###### Debt to Total Assets Ratio

- This ratio measures the percentage of assets financed by debt.
    
- Higher debt to total assets ratio indicates greater risk of insolvency.
    
- Debt financing is riskier than equity financing due to repayment obligations.
    

###### Calculation of Debt to Total Assets

- Debt to total assets ratio = Total debt (current + non-current liabilities) / Total assets.
    
- A higher percentage suggests increased risk of not meeting debt obligations.

##### Understanding Basic Earnings per Share (EPS)

###### Definition

- Basic EPS measures income earned on each common share.
    
- Calculated by dividing income available to common shareholders by the weighted average number of common shares.
    

###### Importance

- Helps compare a company’s performance over different years.
    
- Essential for shareholders to assess investment returns on a per-share basis.
    
- Publicly traded companies are required to report basic EPS in financial statements.
    

###### Calculation Considerations

- Income available to common shareholders equals net income unless preferred shares exist.
    
- If preferred shares are present, preferred dividends must be deducted from net income.
    
- Further details on calculations will be covered in Chapter 11.
    

###### Reporting Requirements

- All publicly traded companies must report at least basic EPS.
    
- Some companies may also report diluted EPS under certain circumstances.
    
- Private corporations under ASPE are not required to report EPS.

##### Understanding the Price-Earnings Ratio

###### Definition

- The Price-Earnings (P-E) ratio is a statistic that compares a company's market share price to its basic earnings per share (EPS).
    
- Calculated by dividing the market price per share by basic earnings per share.
    

###### Purpose

- Helps investors assess how a company's share price relates to its net income.
    
- Reflects investor expectations about future earnings rather than just current earnings.
    
- A higher P-E ratio suggests higher future earning potential as perceived by investors.
    
- Allows for comparison between companies to evaluate share price relative to earnings.
    

###### Aritzia's P-E Ratios

- Market prices for Aritzia's shares were $16.40 in 2019 and $12.54 in 2018.
    
- P-E ratios for Aritzia and Reitmans in 2019 were 33 times and n/a (due to a loss), respectively.
    
- Industry averages were 22.2 times in 2019 and 16.5 times in 2018.
    

###### Investor Expectations

- A higher P-E ratio indicates optimistic investor expectations for future profitability.
    
- Examples: Facebook and Google have higher P-E ratios compared to Ford and General Motors, reflecting differing investor outlooks.
    

###### Interpreting Changes in P-E Ratio

- The P-E ratio measures the relationship between investor expectations and actual company performance.
    
- If both EPS and share price rise equally, the P-E ratio remains unchanged.
    
- If EPS rises by 10% and share price by 25%, the P-E ratio increases, potentially indicating over-optimism.
    
- Aritzia's share price peaked at $19 in late 2016, leading to a high P-E ratio, but later losses caused a drop in share price.
    
- For the year ending March 3, 2019, Aritzia's income and EPS increased, but the share price increase was less significant, resulting in a slight decrease in the P-E ratio.
    

###### Conclusion

- Both Aritzia and Reitmans had P-E ratios above the industry average in 2019, indicating strong investor confidence in their future earnings potential.
    

##### Investment Insights from Benjamin Graham

###### Overview of Benjamin Graham

- Economist and finance professor at Columbia University.
    
- Authored "The Intelligent Investor" in 1949.
    
- Advocated for investing in stable, profitable companies at low share prices.
    

###### Investment Strategies

- Preferred companies with low price-earnings ratios.
    
- Emphasized that low price-earnings ratios indicate cheap shares, not necessarily low growth prospects.
    
- Recommended a current ratio greater than 2:1 for good liquidity and a margin of safety.
