
##### Framework for the Preparation and Presentation of Financial Statements

###### Introduction

- Aritzia and Reitmans follow a standardized framework for financial reporting.
    
- The framework guides decisions on financial information disclosure, reporting formats, and measurement of financial elements.
    

###### Conceptual Framework for Financial Reporting

- Developed by standard-setting bodies in consultation with the accounting profession and business community.
    
- Addresses what to present in financial statements and how to communicate economic events.
    

###### Global Variability in Accounting Standards

- Different countries use varying conceptual frameworks and accounting standards.
    
- Variability arises from differences in legal systems, standard development processes, government requirements, and economic environments.
    
- Global operations face challenges due to these differences, increasing reporting costs.
    

###### International Accounting Standards Board (IASB)

- Responsible for developing International Financial Reporting Standards (IFRS).
    
- Aims to reduce differences in accounting standards globally.
    
- Over 140 countries require IFRS, with Canada adopting it in 2011.
    
- U.S. and China have not fully adopted IFRS, but U.S. regulators accept IFRS financial statements from foreign companies.
    

###### Updates to the Conceptual Framework

- The IASB updated its conceptual framework in 2018.
    
- Applies to any reporting entity preparing financial statements.
    

###### Key Items of the Conceptual Framework

- Objective of general-purpose financial reporting.
    
- Qualitative characteristics of useful financial information.
    
- Going concern assumption.
    
- Elements of financial statements.
    
- Measurement of the elements of financial statements.
    

###### Framework for Publicly Traded vs. Private Companies

- The conceptual framework is similar for publicly traded companies under IFRS and private companies under ASPE.
    
- Differences between the two do not justify separate conceptual frameworks according to the Accounting Standards Board.
    

##### Objective of General-Purpose Financial Reporting

- The primary aim of financial reporting is to provide useful financial information to:
    
    - Existing and potential investors
        
    - Lenders
        
    - Other users
        
- This information aids in decision-making regarding:
    
    - Buying or selling shares
        
    - Lending to the company
        
    - Collecting loans from the company
        
- Decisions are influenced by:
    
    - Company profitability
        
    - Cash flows
        
    - Financial position
        
- Users are also interested in:
    
    - Management’s stewardship of assets
        
    - Future demand and supply forecasts
        
    - Comparative performance with competitors
        
- General-purpose financial statements provide:
    
    - Information about economic resources (assets)
        
    - Claims against these resources (liabilities)
        
    - Effects of transactions and events on resources and claims
        
- Financial statements are prepared using:
    
    - The accrual basis of accounting
        
    - Transactions are recorded in the period they occur, not when cash is exchanged
        

###### Qualitative Characteristics of Useful Financial Information

- Qualitative characteristics help identify useful information for:
    
    - Investors
        
    - Lenders
        
    - Other creditors
        
- These characteristics are divided into:
    
    - **Fundamental Qualitative Characteristics:** Required for financial information to be useful
        
    - **Enhancing Qualitative Characteristics:** Increase the usefulness of already useful information
        

##### Fundamental Qualitative Characteristics of Financial Information

###### Overview

- Two fundamental qualitative characteristics:
    
    - Relevance
        
    - Faithful Representation
        
- Useful for decision-making.
    

###### Relevance

- Influences user decisions.
    
- Types of relevant information:
    
    - Predictive Value: Helps predict future events.
        
    - Confirmatory Value: Confirms or corrects previous predictions.
        
- Example: Current year sales data can confirm sales strategy effectiveness and aid future predictions.
    

###### Materiality

- Component of relevance.
    
- Material information:
    
    - Omission or misstatement could influence decisions.
        
    - Determined by:
        
        - Magnitude (dollar value)
            
        - Nature (what the information relates to)
            
- Example: A bribe may not be significant in amount but is material due to its ethical implications.
    

###### Faithful Representation

- Must represent economic reality.
    
- Requires reporting economic substance over legal form.
    
- Characteristics of faithful representation:
    
    - Complete: No important omissions.
        
    - Neutral: Unbiased representation.
        
    - Free from Material Error: Accurate description without errors.
        
- Example: A product sold with a buy-back agreement should not be recorded as a sale.
    

###### Neutrality and Prudence

- Neutrality achieved through prudence:
    
    - Caution in judgments without being overly conservative.
        

###### Limitations of Financial Information

- Perfect financial information is rarely achievable.
    
- Faithful representation does not guarantee complete accuracy.
    
- Estimates are often required and can be faithfully represented if:
    
    - Clearly labeled as estimates.
        
    - Nature and limitations of the estimating process are discussed.
        

##### Fundamental Qualities of Useful Information

###### Relevance

- Accounting information is relevant if it influences business decisions.
    
- Relevant information has:
    
    - Predictive value: Helps in forming accurate future expectations.
        
    - Confirmatory value: Confirms or corrects prior expectations.
        
- Materiality is a company-specific aspect of relevance.
    
- An item is material if its size is likely to influence investor or creditor decisions.
    

###### Faithful Representation

- Faithful representation means information accurately depicts reality.
    
- To ensure faithful representation, information must be:
    
    - Complete: No important information omitted.
        
    - Neutral: Not biased toward any position.
        
    - Free from error.
        

###### Accounting Matters: Case Study of Fraud

- Users expect financial statements to be representationally faithful.
    
- Fraud example: Alberta Motor Association (AMA) case.
    
    - James Gladden, former vice-president, sentenced for fraud in January 2018.
        
    - Gladden approved fictitious invoices for services from companies he owned.
        
    - Suppressed some payments and misclassified others in expense accounts.
        
    - Received over $8 million through fraudulent payments.
        
    - AMA launched a civil suit and recovered almost $3 million of Gladden’s assets.
        
    - Remaining losses were covered by insurance.
        

##### Enhancing Qualitative Characteristics

###### Overview

- Two fundamental qualities: relevance and faithful representation.
    
- Four enhancing qualities:
    
    - Comparability
        
    - Verifiability
        
    - Timeliness
        
    - Understandability
        

###### Comparability

- Allows users to identify similarities and differences among items.
    
- Enhances usefulness by enabling comparisons over time and across companies.
    

###### Verifiability

- Ensures that independent users can agree on the accuracy of information.
    
- Can be assessed directly (e.g., counting cash) or indirectly (e.g., recalculating outputs).
    
- Public accountants audit financial statements for verification.
    

###### Timeliness

- Information must be available before it loses relevance for decision-making.
    
- Regulatory requirements for public companies:
    
    - Financial statements within 45 days of quarter end.
        
    - Financial statements within 90 days of year end.
        

###### Understandability

- Information should be presented clearly and concisely.
    
- Users with reasonable business knowledge should be able to interpret the information.
    

###### Application of Enhancing Qualitative Characteristics

- Iterative process; no prescribed order for application.
    
- Trade-offs may occur, such as prioritizing timeliness over verifiability.
    

##### Cost Constraint

###### Overview

- Cost associated with ensuring qualitative characteristics in financial statements.
    
- Cost constraint arises when preparation costs exceed the value of the information.
    

###### Benefits vs. Costs

- Benefits of financial reporting should justify the costs of providing and using the information.
    
- Example: Achieving completeness may increase costs without proportional benefits.
    

##### Understanding the Going Concern Assumption

- The going concern assumption is a fundamental principle in accounting.
    
- It posits that a company will continue its operations in the foreseeable future.
    
- Assumption is based on the company's history of profitability and access to financial resources.
    
- If a company is deemed a going concern, assets like equipment are classified as non-current.
    
- If not, assets may be classified as current, indicating potential liquidation.
    

###### Elements of Financial Statements

- Financial statements categorize financial effects of transactions into broad classes.
    
- Key elements include:
    
    - Assets
        
    - Liabilities
        
    - Equity
        
    - Income (including gains)
        
    - Expenses (including losses)
        
- Accounting standards aim for universal measurement and application of these elements.
    

###### Measurement of the Elements

- Foundational accounting principles guide recognition and measurement of financial elements.
    
- Generally Accepted Accounting Principles (GAAP) are widely recognized standards.
    
- In Canada, GAAP has authoritative support from business corporations acts and securities legislation.
    
- Two primary bases of measurement are:
    
    - Historical Cost
        
    - Fair Value
        
- Measurement bases may be referred to interchangeably with principles.
    

###### Historical Cost

- The historical cost basis records assets and liabilities at their acquisition cost.
    
- This applies at the time of purchase and throughout the holding period.
    
- Historical cost and cost are used interchangeably in accounting terminology.
    

##### Understanding Historical Cost and Fair Value in Accounting

###### Historical Cost

- Historical cost and cost are interchangeable terms in accounting.
    
- Example: Land purchased for $3 million is recorded at $3 million.
    
- Even if the land's value increases to $4 million, it remains reported at $3 million.
    
- Land is reported at cost until sold, unless fair value drops below cost.
    

###### Fair Value

- Fair value allows reporting certain assets and liabilities at their current market price.
    
- At acquisition, cost and fair value are typically the same.
    
- Over time, fair value may diverge from historical cost.
    
- Example: Land bought for $100,000 may have a fair value of $120,000 after one year.
    
- Companies may report investments at fair value for reliability and relevance.
    

###### Alternative Terminology

- Fair value, current value, and current cost are used interchangeably.
    

###### Decision-Making Considerations

- Companies assess relevance and faithful representation when choosing between cost and fair value.
    
- Aritzia measures most assets at cost, except certain investments.
    
- Standard setters generally require historical cost for most assets due to reliability concerns.
    
- Fair value is applied mainly for assets traded in active markets, like publicly traded shares and bonds.
