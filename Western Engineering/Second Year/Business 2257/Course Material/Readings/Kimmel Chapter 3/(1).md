##### Analyzing Transactions Using the Accounting Equation

###### Introduction

- The first step in the accounting cycle is analyzing transactions.
    
- Not all transactions affecting a company are recorded.
    
- Example: Hiring a new employee does not constitute a recordable transaction until the employee earns a salary.
    

###### Accounting Transactions

- An accounting transaction occurs when an economic event changes a company's financial position.
    
- Changes must be measurable in terms of assets, liabilities, or shareholders’ equity.
    
- Hiring an employee alone does not affect financial records.
    

###### Decision Tool

- The accounting equation: assets = liabilities + shareholders’ equity helps determine if a transaction has occurred.
    

###### Transaction Identification Process

- Events that should be recorded:
    
    - Purchase of a computer (increases assets)
        
    - Paying rent (decreases cash and shareholders’ equity)
        
- Event that should not be recorded:
    
    - Discussing product design with a potential customer (no immediate financial impact)
        

###### Conclusion

- Transactions affecting financial position must be recorded.
    
- Future potential transactions do not affect current financial records until they materialize.
    

##### Accounting Matters: NHL Signing Bonuses

###### Value Addition of Hiring Players

- Hiring a great hockey player is perceived to add value to an NHL team.
    
- Simply signing a contract does not constitute an accounting transaction.
    
- Value is recognized only when the player starts playing and earning a salary.
    

###### Signing Bonuses

- Signing bonuses can lead to accounting transactions.
    
- Bonuses may be paid upfront or over time, depending on contract terms.
    
- Upfront bonuses change the team's assets, liabilities, and equity.
    

###### Basic Accounting Equation

- The basic accounting equation is: Assets = Liabilities + Shareholders’ Equity.
    
- Shareholders’ equity consists of common shares and retained earnings.
    
- Retained earnings equal net income minus dividends declared.
    

###### Expanded Accounting Equation

- The expanded equation provides a framework for recording transactions.
    
- It includes detailed components of shareholders’ equity.
    
- Transactions must always balance the accounting equation.
    

###### Transaction Effects

- Recorded transactions have a dual effect on the accounting equation.
    
- Increasing an asset requires a corresponding decrease in another asset, an increase in a liability, or an increase in common shares or revenues.
    

##### Impact of Asset Decrease, Liability Increase, or Revenue Growth on Shareholders' Equity

###### Basic Analysis of Transactions

- Services worth $200 provided.
    
- Cash received: $50.
    
- Accounts Receivable: $150 (amount owed).
    
- Shareholders' Equity (Service Revenue) increases by $200.
    

###### Equation Analysis Overview

- Assets = Liabilities + Shareholders' Equity
    
- Revenues abbreviated as "Rev.", expenses as "Exp.", dividends as "Div."
    

###### Transaction Example: Investment in Sierra Corporation

- Date: October 1.
    
- Cash investment: $10,000.
    
- Common Shares issued: 10,000.
    
- Effect on Financial Position:
    
    - Cash increases by $10,000.
        
    - Common Shares increase by $10,000.
        

###### Balance Maintenance

- Transaction results in equal increase in assets and shareholders' equity.
    
- Accounting equation remains balanced.
    
- Investments by shareholders recorded as common shares, not revenue.
    

###### Additional Transaction Example

- New liability: Bank loan payable of $5,000.
    
- Total assets: $15,000.
    
- Liabilities ($5,000) + Shareholders' Equity ($10,000) = Total Assets ($15,000).
    
- Accounting equation remains balanced.
    

##### Recording a Bank Loan Transaction: Sierra and Scotiabank

###### Transaction Details

- Date: October 1
    
- Amount Borrowed: $5,000
    
- Lender: Scotiabank
    
- Loan Type: Bank loan payable with 6% interest
    
- Repayment Date: January 1, 2022
    

###### Basic Analysis

- Asset Increase: Cash increased by $5,000
    
- Liability Increase: Bank Loan Payable increased by $5,000
    

###### Equation Analysis

- Accounting Equation: Assets = Liabilities + Shareholders’ Equity
    
- Assets Column: Cash amount listed
    
- Liabilities Column: Bank Loan Payable listed
    
- Shareholders’ Equity Column: Includes Common Shares, Revenue, Expense, and Dividends
    

###### Transaction Amounts

- Initial Cash: $10,000
    
- Initial Common Shares: $10,000
    
- Transaction 2: Cash and Bank Loan Payable both increased by $5,000
    
- Final Cash Total: $15,000
    
- Final Bank Loan Payable: $5,000
    
- Final Common Shares: $10,000
    

###### Final Totals

- Total Cash: $15,000
    
- Total Bank Loan Payable: $5,000
    
- Total Common Shares: $10,000
    
- Combined Total: $15,000
    

###### Statement of Income

- No effect from this transaction
    

###### Interest Accumulation

- No interest recorded on October 1
    
- Interest will accumulate over the next three months
    

###### Overall Impact

- One asset increases while another decreases by equal amounts
    
- Total assets remain at $15,000
    
- Total liabilities plus shareholders’ equity also remain at $15,000
    

##### Recording Transactions Using Equation Analysis

###### Transaction Overview

- **Date:** October 1
    
- **Transaction:** Sierra purchased equipment by paying $5,000 cash.
    

###### Basic Analysis

- **Asset Changes:**
    
    - Equipment increased by $5,000
        
    - Cash decreased by $5,000
        

###### Accounting Equation

- **Equation:** Assets = Liabilities + Shareholders’ Equity
    
- **Assets:** Cash + Equipment
    
- **Liabilities:** Bank Loan Payable
    
- **Shareholders’ Equity:** Common Shares + Revenue - Expense - Dividends
    

###### Transaction Details

- **Initial Balances:**
    
    - Cash: $15,000
        
    - Bank Loan Payable: $5,000
        
    - Common Shares: $10,000
        
- **After Transaction:**
    
    - Cash: $10,000 (decrease of $5,000)
        
    - Equipment: $5,000 (increase of $5,000)
        
    - Bank Loan Payable: $5,000
        
    - Common Shares: $10,000
        

###### Final Balances

- **Total Assets:** $15,000 (Cash + Equipment)
    
- **Total Liabilities and Equity:** $15,000 (Bank Loan Payable + Common Shares)
    
- **Statement of Income:** No effect
    

###### Revenue and Expense Reporting

- Individual revenues and expenses, such as Rent Expense, are listed under Rev. and Exp. headings.
    
- The specific account affected will be identified in the Statement of Income column.
    
- This step is crucial for future journalizing transactions.
