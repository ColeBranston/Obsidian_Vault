- The accounting equation must always remain balanced: assets = liabilities + shareholders’ equity.

##### T Accounts Overview

- An account consists of three parts:
    
    - Title of the account
        
    - Left side (Debit)
        
    - Right side (Credit)
        
- Terms:
    
    - Debit (Dr.) = Left
        
    - Credit (Cr.) = Right
        
- Also known as a general ledger account.
    
- The entire group of accounts is called the ledger.
    

###### T Account Form

- Basic structure:
    
    - Account name at the top
        
    - Left side for debits (Dr.)
        
    - Right side for credits (Cr.)
        
- Practical account forms include:
    
    - Date
        
    - Explanation
        
    - Reference (Ref.)
        
    - Debit
        
    - Credit
        
    - Balance
        
- T accounts are used for teaching; formal six-column forms are used in practice.
    

###### Normal Balances

- Accounts have normal debit or credit balances based on their type:
    
    - Assets = Debit balance
        
    - Liabilities = Credit balance
        
    - Shareholders’ Equity = Credit balance
        
- Basic accounting equation:
    
    - Assets = Liabilities + Shareholders’ Equity
        
- Normal balance indicates the expected ending balance side of the T account.
    

###### Decision Tool

- Determining normal balance:
    
    - Left side of T = Normal debit balance
        
    - Right side of T = Normal credit balance
        
- To increase an account balance, use the normal balance side; to decrease, use the opposite side.
    

##### Understanding Assets in Accounting

###### Definition and Position

- Assets are located on the left side of the accounting equation.
    
- Asset accounts typically have debit (left-side) balances.
    

###### Recording Increases and Decreases

- Increases in asset accounts are recorded as debits on the left side of a T account.
    
- Decreases in asset accounts are recorded as credits on the right side of a T account.
    
- Normal balance for asset accounts is a debit balance.
    

###### Identifying Errors

- Knowing an account's normal balance helps identify recording errors.
    
- For example, a Land account should have a debit balance; a credit balance indicates an error.
    
- Exceptions exist, such as bank overdrafts, which can result in a credit balance for the Cash account.
    

###### Example: Cash Account of Sierra Corporation

- Cash increases are recorded as debits; decreases are recorded as credits.
    
- Positive amounts represent cash receipts; negative amounts represent cash payments.
    
- The Cash account balance is determined by netting debits and credits.
    
- Sierra Corporation's Cash account shows a debit balance of $11,400, indicating more cash inflows than outflows.
    

##### Understanding Liabilities and Shareholders' Equity

###### Liabilities

- Liabilities are recorded on the right side of the accounting equation.
    
- Liability accounts typically have credit balances.
    
- Increases in liabilities are recorded as credits (right side of T account).
    
- Decreases in liabilities are recorded as debits (left side of T account).
    
- Credits to liability accounts should exceed debits to maintain a normal credit balance.
    
- Illustration 3.25 shows the T account for liabilities with debit for decrease and credit for increase.
    

###### Helpful Hint for Liabilities

- Increases in liability accounts are recorded by credits.
    
- Liability accounts normally maintain credit balances.
    

###### Comparison with Assets

- Liabilities are opposite to assets in the accounting equation.
    
- Increases and decreases in liabilities are recorded in the opposite manner to assets.
    

###### Shareholders' Equity

- Shareholders' equity consists of common shares and retained earnings.
    
- Retained earnings can be divided into revenues, expenses, and dividends declared.
    
- Revenues increase retained earnings, while expenses and dividends decrease it.
    
- Common shares, retained earnings, and revenue accounts are increased by credits.
    
- These accounts are decreased by debits, maintaining a normal credit balance.
    

###### Helpful Hint for Shareholders' Equity

- Increases in common shares, retained earnings, and revenue accounts are recorded by credits.
    
- These accounts typically have normal credit balances.
    

##### Understanding Increases in Shareholders’ Equity

- Common shares, retained earnings, and revenue accounts increase with credits.
    
- These accounts have normal credit balances.
    
- Debits decrease these accounts.
    

###### T-Accounts Overview

- **Common Shares:**
    
    - Debit for decrease
        
    - Credit for increase
        
    - Normal balance: Credit
        
- **Retained Earnings:**
    
    - Debit for decrease
        
    - Credit for increase
        
    - Normal balance: Credit
        
- **Revenues:**
    
    - Debit for decrease
        
    - Credit for increase
        
    - Normal balance: Credit
        

##### Decreases in Shareholders’ Equity

- Expenses and dividends declared decrease retained earnings.
    
- These decreases affect overall shareholders’ equity.
    
- Expenses reduce net income, while revenues increase it.
    

###### Expense and Dividends Declared Accounts

- Expense accounts:
    
    - Increased by debits
        
    - Decreased by credits
        
    - Normal balance: Debit
        
- Dividends Declared accounts:
    
    - Increased by debits
        
    - Decreased by credits
        
    - Normal balance: Debit
        

##### Understanding Normal Balances and Debit/Credit Effects

###### Normal Balances

- **Assets:** Increased by Debits
    
- **Liabilities:** Increased by Credits
    
- **Shareholders’ Equity:**
    
    - **Common Shares:** Increased by Credits
        
    - **Retained Earnings:** Increased by Credits
        
    - **Revenues:** Increased by Credits
        
    - **Expenses:** Increased by Debits
        
    - **Dividends Declared:** Increased by Debits
        

###### Debit and Credit Effects

- Only debits increase debit accounts.
    
- Only credits increase credit accounts.
    
- The normal balance of an account is always on its increase side.
    

###### Accounting Equation

- The accounting equation is: **Assets = Liabilities + Shareholders’ Equity**
    
- Shareholders’ Equity is calculated by adding Common Shares and Retained Earnings.
    
- Retained Earnings is calculated as: **Revenue - Expenses - Dividends Declared**
    

##### Understanding the Accounting Equation and Financial Statement Relationships

###### Assets

- Increased by debits.
    
- Have a normal debit balance.
    

###### Liabilities and Shareholders’ Equity

- Located on the right-hand side of the accounting equation.
    
- Increased by credits.
    
- Have a normal credit balance.
    

###### Components of Shareholders’ Equity

- Divided into common shares and retained earnings.
    
- Both common shares and retained earnings are increased by credits.
    
- Have a normal credit balance.
    

###### Retained Earnings

- Subdivided into revenues, expenses, and dividends declared.
    
- Revenues increase retained earnings and are recorded by credits.
    
- Expenses and dividends declared decrease retained earnings and are recorded by debits.
    
- Revenues have a normal credit balance; expenses and dividends declared have a normal debit balance.
    

###### Double-Entry Accounting System

- Total debits must equal total credits.
    
- Records the dual effect of each transaction in appropriate accounts.
    
- Ensures accurate recording of transactions.
    

###### Financial Statement Reporting

- Assets, liabilities, and shareholders’ equity are reported in financial statements.
    
- Components include common shares, retained earnings, revenues, expenses, and dividends declared.
    

##### Overview of Financial Statements

###### Statement of Income

- Reports revenues and expenses for the period.
    
- Determines net income (or loss).
    
- Items are generally listed from largest to smallest, except for income tax expense, which is reported separately near the end.
    

###### Statement of Changes in Equity

- Shows changes in all shareholders’ equity items, including common shares and retained earnings.
    
- Begins with opening balances from the prior period.
    
- Displays changes during the period to determine ending balances.
    
- Includes net income (or loss) and dividends declared in the retained earnings column.
    

###### Statement of Financial Position

- Reports balances for assets, liabilities, and shareholders’ equity at the end of the period.
    
- Assets must equal liabilities plus shareholders’ equity.
    
- Common shares and retained earnings are reported in the shareholders’ equity section.
    
- Assets and liabilities are classified into current and non-current portions.
    

###### Interrelationships of Financial Statements

- Revenues, expenses, and dividends declared are transferred to retained earnings at the end of the period.
