
##### Journalizing Transactions

###### Overview of the Accounting Cycle

- Analyze transactions
    
- Journalize the transaction
    
- Post
    
- Trial balance
    
- Adjusting entries
    
- Adjusted trial balance
    
- Financial statements
    
- Closing entries
    
- Post-closing trial balance
    

###### Importance of Analyzing Transactions

- Determine if a transaction affects accounts
    
- Source documents provide evidence (e.g., sales receipts, cheques, invoices)
    
- Critical to decide what to record
    

###### Use of Computerized Accounting Systems

- Systems range from simple spreadsheets to sophisticated software
    
- Process all steps in the accounting cycle after transaction analysis
    
- Manual analysis is essential for determining affected accounts
    

###### General Journal Functionality

- Records accounting transactions after analysis
    
- Transactions are recorded chronologically
    
- Shows debit and credit effects on specific accounts
    
- Essential for maintaining a complete record of transactions
    

###### Contributions of the General Journal

- Discloses complete effects of transactions in one place
    
- Provides a chronological record
    
- Helps prevent and locate errors through comparison of debit and credit amounts
    

###### Journalizing Process

- Entering transaction data is known as journalizing
    
- Includes a debit–credit analysis to identify account effects
    
- Illustrated through examples, such as the issuance of common shares
    

##### Debit-Credit Analysis of Sierra Corporation Investment

###### Transaction Overview

- Date: October 1
    
- Transaction: Cash investment of $10,000 in exchange for 10,000 common shares
    

###### Steps of Analysis

- **Basic Analysis:**
    
    - Asset Cash increased by $10,000
        
    - Shareholders' Equity account Common Shares increased by $10,000
        
- **Equation Analysis:**
    
    - Accounting Equation: Assets = Liabilities + Shareholders’ Equity
        
    - Assets: Cash +$10,000
        
    - Shareholders' Equity: Common Shares +$10,000
        
    - Liabilities: None
        
- **Debit-Credit Analysis:**
    
    - Debit Cash $10,000 (increases assets)
        
    - Credit Common Shares $10,000 (increases equity)
        
- **Journal Entry:**
    
    - Date: October 1
        
    - Debit: Cash $10,000
        
    - Credit: Common Shares $10,000
        
    - Description: Issued common shares
        

###### Journal Entry Features

- Date is recorded in the Date column
    
- Debited account is listed first, credited account is indented below
    
- Debits recorded in the left column, credits in the right column
    
- Brief explanation of the transaction provided
    
- No reference included in this illustration
    

###### Types of Journal Entries

- Simple Journal Entry: Involves one debit and one credit
    
- Compound Entry: Involves three or more accounts
    
- Total debits must equal total credits
    

###### Helpful Hint

- Use specific account titles in journal entries
    
- Maintain consistency in account titles for accurate financial reporting
    
- Explanations can be omitted unless instructed otherwise

###### Transaction Overview

- **August 1:** Common shares issued for $25,000 cash.
    
- **August 3:** Equipment purchased on account for $7,000.
    
- **August 9:** Services performed for $8,000; $2,000 cash received, $6,000 on account.
    
- **August 10:** Rent paid for August, $850.
    
- **August 29:** Customers paid $4,000 of the balance due.
    

###### Transaction Details

###### August 1: Common Shares Issued

- **Basic Analysis:** Cash and Common Shares increased by $25,000.
    
- **Journal Entry:**
    
    - Debit Cash: $25,000
        
    - Credit Common Shares: $25,000
        

###### August 3: Equipment Purchased

- **Basic Analysis:** Equipment increased by $7,000; Accounts Payable increased by $7,000.
    
- **Journal Entry:**
    
    - Debit Equipment: $7,000
        
    - Credit Accounts Payable: $7,000
        

###### August 9: Services Performed

- **Basic Analysis:** Cash increased by $2,000; Accounts Receivable increased by $6,000; Service Revenue increased by $8,000.
    
- **Journal Entry:**
    
    - Debit Cash: $2,000
        
    - Debit Accounts Receivable: $6,000
        
    - Credit Service Revenue: $8,000
        

###### August 10: Rent Paid

- **Basic Analysis:** Cash decreased by $850; Rent Expense increased by $850.
    
- **Journal Entry:**
    
    - Debit Rent Expense: $850
        
    - Credit Cash: $850
        

###### August 29: Customer Payments

- **Basic Analysis:** Cash increased by $4,000; Accounts Receivable decreased by $4,000.
    
- **Journal Entry:**
    
    - Debit Cash: $4,000
        
    - Credit Accounts Receivable: $4,000
        

##### Transaction Analysis and Posting

###### Basic Analysis

- Cash increased by $4,000.
    
- Accounts Receivable decreased by $4,000.
    

###### Equation Analysis

- Assets = Liabilities + Shareholders’ Equity
    
- Cash + Accounts Receivable + Equipment = Accounts Payable + Common Shares + Retained Earnings
    

###### Transaction Date: August 29

- Rev. - Exp. - Div. = +$4,000 -$4,000
    
- Debit: Cash $4,000 (increases assets).
    
- Credit: Accounts Receivable $4,000 (decreases assets).
    

###### Journal Entry - August 29

- Debit Cash: $4,000
    
- Credit Accounts Receivable: $4,000
    

###### Transaction Date: August 31

- Dividends of $1,000 declared and paid to shareholders.
    
- Cash decreased by $1,000.
    
- Dividends Declared increased by $1,000, reducing retained earnings and shareholders’ equity.
    

###### Equation Analysis

- Assets = Liabilities + Shareholders’ Equity
    
- Cash + Accounts Receivable + Equipment = Payable + Common Shares + Retained Earnings
    

###### Transaction Date: August 31

- Rev. + Exp. + Div. = -$1,000 -$1,000
    
- Debit: Dividends Declared $1,000 (increases dividends).
    
- Credit: Cash $1,000 (decreases assets).
    

###### Journal Entry - August 31

- Debit Dividends Declared: $1,000
    
- Credit Cash: $1,000
    

###### Posting Transactions

- Step 3 in the accounting cycle: Transfer journal entries to the general ledger.
    
- The ledger contains all asset, liability, shareholders’ equity, dividends declared, revenue, and expense accounts.
    
- Accounts are organized for easy identification, often using a computerized accounting system.
    
- The ledger is arranged in the order of financial statement presentation: assets, liabilities, shareholders’ equity, revenue, and expenses.
    

##### Sierra Corporation—Chart of Accounts Overview

###### Definition

- The chart of accounts is a framework for the accounting information system.
    
- It lists general ledger accounts and their corresponding account numbers.
    

###### Variability

- The number and type of accounts vary by company size, complexity, and business type.
    
- Management may choose to consolidate or separate accounts based on detail preferences.

# Understanding Posting in Accounting

##### Overview of Posting

- Posting is the transfer of journal entries from the general journal to the general ledger.
    
- It accumulates the effects of journalized transactions in individual accounts.
    
- Debits and credits are recorded in the appropriate columns of the general ledger.
    

###### Posting Procedure

- Entries should be posted in chronological order.
    
- All debits and credits of one journal entry must be posted before moving to the next.
    
- Posting should occur at least monthly to keep the general ledger updated.
    
- In computerized systems, posting often happens simultaneously with journal entry preparation.
    

###### Accounting Cycle Steps

- The first three steps are analyze, journalize, and post transactions.
    
- Analysis and entry of transactions must be performed by a person, even in computerized systems.
    
- The main difference between manual and computerized systems lies in the posting process.
    

##### Example: Ahair Ltd. Posting Exercise

- Opening balances as of April 30:
    
    - Cash: $10,000
        
    - Accounts Receivable: $5,000
        
    - Equipment: $7,500
        
    - Accounts Payable: $6,000
        
- Journal entries recorded in May:
    
    - May 1: Accounts Payable $5,000; Cash $5,000 (Partial payment on account)
        
    - May 4: Equipment $4,800; Bank Loan Payable $4,800 (Purchased equipment)
        
    - May 6: Supplies $600; Cash $600 (Purchased supplies)
        
    - May 15: Cash $2,700; Accounts Receivable $2,700 (Received partial payment)
        

###### Instructions for Posting

- Set up T accounts and enter April 30 balances.
    
- Post May journal entries to the general ledger.
    
- Determine ending balances in each account.
    

###### Action Plan for Posting

- Transfer journalized debits and credits to T accounts in the general ledger.
    
- Include opening balances in the accounts.
    
- Post debits on the left and credits on the right side of T accounts.
    
- Arrange ledger accounts in statement order.
    
- Calculate ending balances by netting total debits and credits.
