###### Limitations of a Trial Balance

- The debit and credit totals must agree; discrepancies indicate potential errors in journalizing or posting.
    
- Common errors include unequal amounts in journal entries or incorrect transfers to the general ledger.
    
- If the trial balance does not balance, errors must be identified and corrected before proceeding.
    

###### Error Identification Tips

- Carefully retrace all steps in the accounting cycle.
    
- Apply specific tips to quickly find errors based on the difference between total debits and credits.
    

##### Error Detection in Trial Balance

- If the error is an amount like $1, $100, or $1,000, re-add the trial balance columns.
    
- If the error can be evenly divided by two:
    
    - Scan the trial balance for a balance equal to half the error entered in the wrong column.
        
- If the error can be evenly divided by nine:
    
    - Retrace account balances on the trial balance to check for incorrect copying from the ledger.
        
    - Example: A balance of $12 listed as $21 indicates a $9 transposition error.
        
- If the error cannot be evenly divided by two or nine:
    
    - Scan the ledger for an omitted account balance matching the error amount.
        
    - Check the journal for any omitted postings of the error amount.
        

Note: These methods may not always identify the error, especially if multiple errors exist.

###### Computerized Systems and Trial Balance

- In computerized systems, the trial balance is typically balanced due to:
    
    - Restrictions on entering unbalanced journal entries.
        
    - Automatic posting of journal entries and preparation of the trial balance.
        
    - Programming to flag normal balance violations and generate error reports.
        

###### Limitations of Trial Balance

- A trial balance does not guarantee that all transactions are recorded or that the general ledger is correct.
    
- Possible errors even when trial balance totals agree:
    
    - A transaction is not journalized.
        
    - A correct journal entry is not posted.
        
    - A journal entry is posted twice.
        
    - Incorrect accounts are used in journalizing or posting.
        
    - Errors that cancel each other’s effects are made in recording transaction amounts.
        
- Equal debits and credits can still result in a balanced trial balance despite errors.
    

###### Ethics Note

- Auditors differentiate between errors and fraud:
    
    - Errors are unintentional misstatements.
        
    - Fraud is an intentional misstatement, considered unethical and often illegal.

##### Accounting Cycle Steps 1–4 Overview

###### Step 1: Identify Transactions

- Recognize economic events affecting the business.
    
- Gather source documents for evidence.
    

###### Step 2: Record Transactions

- Enter transactions into the journal.
    
- Use double-entry accounting for accuracy.
    

###### Step 3: Post to Ledger

- Transfer journal entries to the general ledger.
    
- Organize accounts for easy tracking.
    

###### Step 4: Prepare Trial Balance

- Summarize all account balances.
    
- Ensure total debits equal total credits.
    

##### Accounting Steps and Trial Balance Preparation

###### Sequential Steps in Accounting

- Steps 1 and 2: May occur daily during the accounting period.
    
- Step 3: Can occur daily; however, most companies post monthly unless using computerized systems.
    
- Step 4: Performed periodically (monthly, quarterly, or annually).
