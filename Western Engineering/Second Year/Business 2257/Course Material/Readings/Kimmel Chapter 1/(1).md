# Uses and Users of Accounting Information

## Learning Objective

Identify the uses and users of accounting information.

## Importance of Accounting

Accounting serves as an information system that identifies, records, and communicates the economic events of an organization. It is crucial for:

- Providing transparent, reliable, and accurate financial reporting.
    
- Being recognized as "the language of business."

## Users of Accounting Information

Users can be categorized into two main types:

- **Internal Users:** Individuals who manage organizations and have access to internal accounting information.
    
- **External Users:** Individuals or entities outside the organization who rely on financial information for decision-making.
    

## Internal Users

Internal users include company officers, managers, and directors across various functions. They utilize accounting information to make informed decisions. Key questions they may ask include:

##### Questions Asked by Internal Users

- **Finance:**
    
    - Is there enough cash to pay the bills?
        
    - Do we need to borrow more money?
        
- **Marketing:**
    
    - What price should we sell tablets for to maximize profits?
        
    - Did our advertising campaign increase sales?
        
- **Human Resources:**
    
    - How many employees can we afford to hire this year?
        
    - Can we afford to negotiate salary increases?
        
- **Production:**
    
    - Which product line is the most profitable?
        
    - Should we contract out some of our production or distribution operations?
        

##### Understanding Accounting Information for Users

###### Importance of Timely Accounting Information

- Users require detailed accounting information when needed.
    
- Internal reports include:
    
    - Financial comparisons of operating alternatives
        
    - Projections of income from new sales campaigns
        
    - Analyses of sales costs
        
    - Forecasts of cash needs
        
- Summarized financial information is presented in financial statements for both internal and external use.
    

###### External Users of Accounting Information

- External users do not manage the company and have limited access to accounting information.
    
- Types of external users include:
    
    - Investors (also known as shareholders)
        
    - Creditors (also known as lenders), including bankers and suppliers
        
- Primary users of financial accounting information are investors and creditors.
    

###### Questions from External Users

- Investors may ask:
    
    - Should I purchase shares of this company?
        
    - What is the return from my investment?
        
- Creditors may inquire:
    
    - Will the company be able to pay its debts as they come due?
        
    - How does the amount of funds invested by shareholders compare with the amount borrowed from creditors?
        

##### Understanding Users of Accounting Information

###### External Users

- Investors: Assess company performance and share price trends.
    
- Creditors: Evaluate the company's ability to repay loans.
    
- Potential Employees: Analyze financial and non-financial data for job prospects.
    
- Current Employees: Use financial information for salary and benefit negotiations.
    
- Tax Authorities: Review financial statements for tax assessments.
    

###### Internal Users

- Chief Financial Officer: Manages financial operations and cash flow.
    
- Production Manager: Analyzes labor costs and production efficiency.
    
- Marketing Manager: Evaluates sales trends across demographics and regions.
    

###### Data Analytics in Decision Making

- Utilizes vast data collected by accounting systems.
    
- Employs software and statistics for informed business decisions.
    
- Increasingly common across various types of companies.
    

###### Importance of Ethical Standards

- Financial information must be prepared ethically for user value.
    
- Ethical decision-making is crucial for reliable accounting information.
    
- Most business professionals adhere to legal and responsible practices.
    
- Accountants follow extensive rules of professional conduct.
    
- Companies often have codes of conduct promoting ethical behavior.

##### Forms of Business Organization

###### Primary Forms

- Sole Proprietorship: Owned by one individual, simple to establish.
    
- Partnership: Owned by two or more individuals, shared responsibilities.
    
- Corporation: Separate legal entity, limited liability for owners.
    
- Limited Liability Company (LLC): Combines benefits of partnership and corporation.

###### Proprietorships

- Owned by one person (proprietor).
    
- Simple to set up and provides full control to the owner.
    
- Requires a small amount of capital to start.
    
- Owner receives all income and bears all losses.
    
- Unlimited liability for business debts.
    
- No legal distinction between the owner and the business.
    
- Life of the business is limited to the owner's life.
    
- Income reported as self-employment income on personal tax return.
    
- Business records must be kept separate from personal records.
    

###### Reporting Entity Concept

- Economic activities of the business must be distinct from personal activities.
    
- Aims to ensure financial statements accurately represent business activities.
    
- Applies to proprietorships, partnerships, and corporations.
    

###### Common Examples

- Small service businesses (e.g., hair salons, plumbers, mechanics).
    
- Small-scale farms.
    
- Small retail stores.
    

##### Partnerships and Corporations Overview

###### Partnerships

- A partnership is a business owned by multiple individuals.
    
- Similar to a proprietorship but with more than one owner.
    
- Formed to combine resources or skills for starting or expanding a business.
    
- Requires a written partnership agreement that includes:
    
    - Formation details
        
    - Partners’ contributions
        
    - Income and loss sharing
        
    - Withdrawal provisions
        
    - Dispute resolution
        
    - Liquidation procedures
        
- More complex and costly to establish than a proprietorship.
    
- Partners generally have unlimited liability for partnership debts.
    
- Limited liability options may be available for certain partners.
    
- Income is reported as self-employment income on personal tax returns.
    
- Partnership records must be kept separate from personal activities.
    
- Commonly used in professional service businesses (e.g., lawyers, doctors, architects).
    

###### Corporations

- A corporation is a separate legal entity owned by shareholders.
    
- Most complex and costly business structure to establish.
    
- Shareholders receive shares as proof of ownership.
    
- Individuals can invest small amounts to become shareholders.
    
- Example: The North West Company is a corporation.
    

###### Alternative Terminology

- Shares are also referred to as stock.
    

##### Understanding Shareholder Dynamics in Corporations

###### Separate Reporting Entities

- Shareholders and corporations are distinct entities under the reporting entity concept.
    
- Personal financial details of shareholders are not included in corporate financial statements.
    

###### Indefinite Life of Corporations

- Corporations exist independently of their owners, continuing indefinitely.
    
- Not affected by the withdrawal, death, or incapacity of shareholders.
    
- Example: Hudson’s Bay Company, established in 1670, is North America's oldest corporation.
    

###### Liability Considerations

- Proprietorships and partnerships expose owners to personal liability for business debts.
    
- Shareholders typically enjoy limited liability, risking only their investment in shares.
    
- Limited liability is a key advantage of corporate structure.
    

###### Advantages of Corporations

- Indefinite life, ease of ownership transfer, and limited liability facilitate capital raising.
    
- Corporations may benefit from favorable income tax treatment compared to other business forms.
    
- Professional advice on taxation is recommended before choosing a business structure.
    

###### Corporate Revenue vs. Other Business Forms

- Corporations generate significantly higher revenue than proprietorships and partnerships.
    
- Major Canadian corporations include Alimentation Couche-Tard, Royal Bank of Canada, and Suncor.
    
- Top 15 Canadian corporations reported annual revenues between $34.7 billion and $78.8 billion.
    

###### Public Corporations

- Public corporations have shares traded on stock exchanges like the Toronto Stock Exchange (TSX).
    
- They have thousands of shareholders, making their shares widely held.
    
- Example: Royal Bank of Canada has over 1.4 billion shares purchased.
    

###### Management Structure

- Shareholders do not directly manage the corporation.
    
- Management is conducted through a board of directors elected by shareholders.
    
- The board sets strategic objectives and hires executives, such as the CEO, for daily operations.
    

##### Understanding Public and Private Corporations

###### Public Corporations

- Required to publish financial statements quarterly and annually.
    
- Financial statements available on their websites and SEDAR (System for Electronic Document Analysis and Retrieval).
    
- Example: North West's financial statements are accessible on SEDAR and in Appendix A of this textbook.
    

###### Private Corporations

- Issue shares not available to the general public; shares are "closely held."
    
- Face challenges in raising capital compared to public corporations.
    
- Examples of large private corporations include:
    
    - Irving Group of Companies
        
    - Jim Pattison Group
        
    - McCain Foods
        
- Pattison Group reported annual revenue of $10.1 billion, comparable to top public corporations.
    
- Private corporations are not obligated to disclose financial statements publicly.
    
- Sobeys is a notable private company that discloses financial information due to outstanding public debt.

- Examples of companies that evolved from proprietorships to corporations:

##### Generally Accepted Accounting Principles for Business Organizations

###### Financial Information Disclosure

- Businesses follow accounting rules and practices for financial reporting.
    
- These rules are known as Generally Accepted Accounting Principles (GAAP).
    

###### Alternative Terminology

- Accounting principles are also referred to as accounting standards or accounting policies.
    

###### Differences in GAAP by Business Type

- Publicly traded corporations must use International Financial Reporting Standards (IFRS).
    
- Private corporations can choose between IFRS or Accounting Standards for Private Enterprises (ASPE).
    
- ASPE aims to reduce complexity and cost for private companies.
    

###### Reasons for Private Companies to Adopt IFRS

- Considering accessing public debt or equity markets.
    
- Desiring comparability with competitors using IFRS.
    
- Having foreign subsidiaries that require IFRS compliance.
    

###### Financial Reporting for Proprietorships and Partnerships

- Generally follow ASPE for external reporting.
    
- Often prepare financial statements for internal use only, without adhering to specific standards.

###### Types of Business Activity

Businesses engage in three main types of activities:

- **Financing:**
    
    - Involves cash inflows and outflows.
        
    - Example: Borrowing funds or distributing dividends.
        
- **Investing:**
    
    - Includes cash outflows for assets like buildings and equipment.
        
    - Example: Investments in new stores and renovations.
        
- **Operating:**
    
    - Generates cash inflows from business operations.
        
    - Example: Revenue from sales used to fund dividends and investments.
        

##### Financing Activities

- **Capital Requirements:** Capital is essential for starting a business.
    
- **Methods of Raising Capital:**
    
    - Equity Financing: Issuing shares for cash or assets.
        
    - Debt Financing: Borrowing money from creditors.
        
- **Initial Share Issuance:**
    
    - First transaction involves issuing shares to shareholders.
        
    - North West issued common shares to the public in 1992.
        
    - Common shares represent ownership in the company.
        
- **Shareholder Financing:**
    
    - Shareholders do not provide all financing; creditors contribute significantly.
        
    - Shareholders have a residual claim on assets after debts are repaid.
        
    - Companies are not obligated to repurchase shares once issued.
        
- **Dividends:**
    
    - Regular payments to shareholders if profitable.
        
    - Dividends can be in cash or other forms, declared by the board.
        
    - North West paid $62,329 thousand in dividends in 2019.
        
    - Dividend payments result in cash outflow.
        
- **Share Repurchases:**
    
    - Corporations may repurchase previously issued shares.
        
    - Requires board and shareholder agreement.
        
    - North West did not repurchase shares in 2019.
        
- **Debt Financing:**
    
    - Corporations can borrow through various means.
        
    - Creditors are individuals or companies owed money.
        
    - Liabilities represent obligations to transfer resources.
        
- **Types of Liabilities:**
    
    - Operating Line of Credit: Pre-arranged bank loan for cash shortfalls.
        
    - Short-term Loans: Also known as notes payable.
        
    - Long-term Debt: Includes mortgages, bonds, and finance leases.
        

##### Understanding Borrowing and Debt Management

###### Cash Flow from Borrowing

- Borrowing funds results in cash inflow.
    
- Repayment of debt leads to cash outflow.
    
- In 2019, North West's debt increased by $44,785 thousand.
    

###### Creditor vs. Shareholder Claims

- Creditors have a legal right to repayment with interest.
    
- Repayment schedules are agreed upon at the time of borrowing.
    
- Nonpayment may result in asset liquidation to settle debts.
    

###### Interest and Dividends Treatment under IFRS

- Interest payments can be classified as financing or operating activities.
    
- Regardless of classification, interest payments are cash outflows.
    
- Dividends can also be classified similarly but are treated differently.
    
- Interest affects net income; dividends do not.
    

###### North West's Financial Activities in 2019

- North West paid $12,254 thousand in interest.
    
- Interest was reported as a financing activity.
