
# Sierra's Statement of Cash Flows Overview

## Overall Cash Increase

- Cash increased by $11,400 during the month.

## Operating Activities

- Generated $1,900 in cash.
    
- Insufficient to cover investing activities.
    

## Investing Activities

- Required $5,000 in cash.
    

## Financing Activities

- Generated $14,500 in cash.
    
- Offset the shortfall from operating and investing activities.
    

## Net Cash Flow

- Cash inflows exceeded outflows, resulting in a net increase of $11,400.
    

## Decision Tool

- Evaluate cash from operating activities against investing and financing needs.
    

## Importance of Cash Management

- Critical for understanding cash sources and uses.
    
- Answers key questions about cash flow during the period:
    
    - Where did cash come from?
        
    - How was cash used?
        
    - What was the change in cash balance?
        

## Types of Activities

### Operating Activities

- Activities to generate net income.
    
- Desirable to have a positive cash balance.
    

### Investing Activities

- Involves purchase/sale of long-lived assets.
    
- Typically uses cash for growing companies.
    

### Financing Activities

- Includes borrowing, repaying, issuing shares, and paying dividends.
    
- Often provides cash for growing companies.

###### Interrelationships Between Financial Statements

- Financial statements are interrelated, with results from one affecting others.
    
- The statement of changes in equity relies on the statement of income.
    
- Ending balances of shareholders’ equity are reflected in the statement of financial position.
    
- The statement of cash flows shows changes in cash, aligning with the statement of financial position.

###### Elements of an Annual Report

- Financial Statements
    
- Management Discussion and Analysis
    
- Independent Auditor’s Report
    
- Notes to the Financial Statements
    

##### Management Discussion and Analysis Overview

- MD&A is a key component of the annual report, offering management's insights on financial results.
    
- Provides context for interpreting financial data.
    
- Includes a forward-looking perspective on future operations and results.
    
- Accompanied by other important financial information such as:
    
    - Statement of management responsibility for financial statements.
        
    - Auditor’s report.
        
    - Financial statements for at least two years.
        
    - Explanatory notes to financial statements.
        
    - Historical summary of key financial ratios and indicators.
        

###### Auditor’s Report

- Prepared by an independent auditor appointed by shareholders.
    
- Provides an opinion on the fairness of financial statements in accordance with applicable accounting principles (IFRS for public companies).
    
- Identifies key audit matters, including:
    
    - Significant risks of misstatement.
        
    - Financial statement items involving significant management estimates.
        
    - Impact of significant events or transactions on the audit.
        
- Reading the auditor’s report offers important context before reviewing financial statements.
    

###### Notes to the Financial Statements

- Accompany every set of financial statements and are integral to understanding them.
    
- Clarify financial statements and provide additional details.
    
- Information may not always be quantifiable, including:
    
    - Descriptions of significant accounting policies and methods.
        
    - Explanations of uncertainties and contingencies.
        
    - Statistics and details too extensive for the main statements.
        
- Essential for understanding a company’s operating performance.
