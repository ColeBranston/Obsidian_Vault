###### Temporary Accounts

Temporary accounts are used to record activities for a specific accounting period and reset at the end of that period. They include:

- **Revenues:** Normal Balance - Credit
    
- **Expenses:** Normal Balance - Debit
    
- **Dividends Declared:** Normal Balance - Debit
    

###### Permanence of Accounts

Permanently held accounts carry their balances into future periods. They include:

- **Assets:** Normal Balance - Debit
    
- **Liabilities:** Normal Balance - Credit
    
- **Shareholders’ Equity:**
    
    - Common Shares: Normal Balance - Credit
        
    - Retained Earnings: Normal Balance - Credit
        

###### Closing Entries

At the end of the accounting period, temporary account balances are transferred to the Retained Earnings account through closing entries. This process updates the Retained Earnings to reflect the end-of-period balance.

###### Accounting Equation Before Closing Entries

The accounting equation before closing entries is:

Assets = Liabilities + Shareholders’ Equity

It includes:

- Common Shares
    
- Retained Earnings (beginning balance)
    
- Revenues
    
- Expenses
    
- Dividends Declared
    

##### Understanding Closing Entries and Retained Earnings

###### Temporary Accounts Before Closing

- Temporary accounts include revenues, expenses, and dividends declared.
    
- These accounts maintain balances until closed.
    

###### Retained Earnings Before Closing

- Retained Earnings may have a zero balance in the first year of operations.
    
- In subsequent years, it carries forward the ending balance from the prior period.
    

###### Post-Closing Entries

- After closing entries, Retained Earnings reflects the end-of-period balance.
    
- This balance aligns with the statement of changes in equity and the statement of financial position.
    

###### Accounting Equation After Closing

- The accounting equation remains: Assets = Liabilities + Shareholders’ Equity.
    
- Permanent accounts report ending balances after closing.
    

###### Impact of Closing Entries

- Closing entries reset temporary accounts to zero.
    
- Retained Earnings is updated to its ending balance.
    
- Temporary accounts are ready to accumulate data for the next period.
    

###### Closing Process Overview

- Revenue and expense accounts can be closed directly to Retained Earnings.
    
- However, they are typically closed to a temporary account called Income Summary.
    
- Income Summary helps determine net income or loss before transferring to Retained Earnings.
    
- Income Summary is opened and closed on the last day of the accounting period.
    

###### Closing Entries Required

- Four closing entries are necessary to close the temporary accounts.
    

##### Closing Entries for Temporary Accounts

###### 1. Close Revenue Accounts to Income Summary

- Debit each revenue account by its balance.
    
- Credit the Income Summary account for total revenues.
    
- Result: All revenue accounts have zero balances.
    

###### 2. Close Expense Accounts to Income Summary

- Debit the Income Summary account for total expenses.
    
- Credit each expense account by its balance.
    
- Result: All expense accounts have zero balances.
    

###### 3. Close Income Summary to Retained Earnings

- Debit Income Summary for its balance (or credit if there is a loss).
    
- Credit Retained Earnings (or debit if there is a loss).
    
- Result: Income Summary account balance is zero.
    

###### 4. Close Dividends Declared to Retained Earnings

- Credit the Dividends Declared account to close it.
    
- Debit Retained Earnings for the balance in Dividends Declared.
    
- Result: Dividends Declared account balance is zero.
    
- Note: Dividends declared are not expenses and do not affect net income.
    

###### Timing of Closing Entries

- Performed after financial statements are prepared.
    
- Typically done at the end of the annual accounting period.
    
- Can be prepared from the general ledger or adjusted trial balance.
