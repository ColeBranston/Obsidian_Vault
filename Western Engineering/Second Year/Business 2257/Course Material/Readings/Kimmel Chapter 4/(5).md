##### Post-Closing Trial Balance Overview

###### Definition

- The post-closing trial balance is the final step in the accounting cycle.
    
- It is prepared after all closing entries are journalized and posted.
    
- It lists all permanent accounts and their balances.
    

###### Purpose

- To prove the equality of permanent account balances carried into the next accounting period.
    
- Only permanent accounts are included, as temporary accounts will have zero balances.

##### Accrual Basis of Accounting

- Definition: Recognizes revenue when earned and expenses when incurred
    
- Reasons for Adjusting Entries:
    
    - To match revenues with expenses
        
    - To ensure accurate financial reporting
        

##### Review of Learning Objectives

###### 1. Accrual Basis of Accounting

- Revenues are recognized when earned, regardless of cash receipt.
    
- Expenses are recognized when incurred, regardless of cash payment.
    
- Differs from cash basis accounting, which records events only when cash is exchanged.
    
- Revenue recognition involves:
    
    - Increase in economic resources (assets or decrease in liabilities).
        
    - Satisfaction of performance obligations.
        
- IFRS five-step model for revenue recognition:
    
    - Identify the contract.
        
    - Identify performance obligations.
        
    - Determine transaction price.
        
    - Allocate transaction price to obligations.
        
    - Recognize revenue upon satisfaction of obligations.
        
- ASPE recognizes revenue when services are provided, measurable, and collection is assured.
    
- Expenses are linked to revenue recognition and recognized in the same period.
    
- Adjusting entries ensure accurate reporting of assets, liabilities, revenues, and expenses.
    
- Types of adjusting entries:
    
    - Prepayments: Adjust for asset costs expensed or deferred revenue recognized.
        
    - Accruals: Record unpaid expenses or unreceived revenues.
        

###### 2. Adjusting Entries for Prepayments

- Prepayments recorded as assets or liabilities.
    
- Adjusting entry for prepaid expenses:
    
    - Debit expense account.
        
    - Credit asset or contra asset account.
        
- Adjusting entry for deferred revenues:
    
    - Debit deferred revenue liability account.
        
    - Credit revenue account.
        

###### 3. Adjusting Entries for Accruals

- Accruals record expenses and revenues for the current period not previously recognized.
    
- Adjusting entry for accrued expenses:
    
    - Debit expense account.
        
    - Credit liability (payable) account.
        
- Adjusting entry for accrued revenues:
    
    - Debit asset (receivable) account.
        
    - Credit revenue account.
        

###### 4. Adjusted Trial Balance and Financial Statements

- Preparation of an adjusted trial balance ensures all accounts are accurate.
    
- Financial statements reflect the adjusted balances for reporting purposes.
    

##### Adjusted Trial Balance and Financial Statements

###### Adjusted Trial Balance

- Shows balances of all accounts at the end of an accounting period.
    
- Includes adjusted account balances.
    
- Demonstrates that total debits equal total credits.
    
- Facilitates the preparation of financial statements.
    

###### Closing Entries

- Update the Retained Earnings account to its end-of-period balance.
    
- Reset temporary accounts (revenue, expense, dividends declared) to zero for the new accounting period.
    
- Close individual revenue and expense accounts to the Income Summary account.
    
- Close the Income Summary account to Retained Earnings.
    
- Close the Dividends Declared account directly to Retained Earnings.
    

###### Post-Closing Trial Balance

- Lists only permanent accounts (statement of financial position accounts).
    
- Account balances are carried forward to the next accounting period.
    
- Proves the equality of total debits and total credits.
