
##### Understanding Deferred Revenues

- Deferred revenues occur when cash is received before goods or services are provided.
    
- Revenue is not recorded until it is earned; instead, a liability is created.
    
- Deferred revenues indicate that performance obligations have not yet been fulfilled.
    
- Examples include:
    
    - Magazine subscriptions
        
    - Rent
        
    - Customer deposits
        
    - Gift cards
        
    - Airline ticket sales
        
    - Tuition fees
        

###### Alternative Terminology

- Deferred revenues are also referred to as unearned revenues.
    
- They are the opposite of prepaid expenses.
    

###### Accounting Treatment

- When cash is received:
    
    - Asset account (Cash) is debited (increased).
        
    - Liability account (Deferred Revenue) is credited (increased).
        
- Adjusting entries are made when revenue is earned:
    
    - Liability account (Deferred Revenue) is debited (decreased).
        
    - Revenue account is credited (increased).
        

###### Examples of Deferred Revenue Treatment

- Airlines treat ticket sales as deferred revenue until the flight is completed.
    
- Retailers treat gift card sales as deferred revenue until redeemed.
    
- Universities consider tuition fees as deferred revenue until the academic term starts.
    

###### Journal Entries

- Initial entry for deferred revenue:
    
    - Debit Cash (increase asset).
        
    - Credit Deferred Revenue (increase liability).
        
- Adjusting entry for earned revenue:
    
    - Debit Deferred Revenue (decrease liability).
        
    - Credit Revenue (increase revenue).
        

##### Deferred Revenues

###### Overview

- Liabilities are overstated until adjustments are made.
    
- Revenues and net income are understated, affecting retained earnings and shareholders’ equity.
    

###### Sierra Example

- Sierra received $1,200 from Knox Ltd. on October 19 for advertising services.
    
- Payment was credited to Deferred Revenue, showing a balance of $1,200 on October 31.
    
- After review, $400 worth of services were performed, necessitating revenue recognition in October.
    

###### Adjustment Process

- Adjustment on October 31: $400 of revenue earned from advance payments.
    
- Five steps in the adjustment process:
    
    - Basic Analysis: Decrease Deferred Revenue by $400; increase Service Revenue by $400.
        
    - Equation Analysis: Assets = Liabilities + Shareholders’ Equity.
        
    - Debit-Credit Analysis: Debit Deferred Revenue $400; Credit Service Revenue $400.
        
    - Journal Entry:
        
        - Date: October 31
            
        - Debit: Deferred Revenue $400
            
        - Credit: Service Revenue $400
            
        - Description: To record revenue earned.
            
    - Posting: Update Deferred Revenue and Service Revenue T-accounts.
        

###### Post-Adjustment Balances

- Deferred Revenue balance: $800, representing future services.
    
- Service Revenue total for October: $20,400.
    
- Without adjustment:
    
    - Revenues and net income understated by $400.
        
    - Liabilities overstated by $400.
        
    - Shareholders’ equity understated by $400.
        

###### Conclusion

- Adjustments are crucial for accurate financial reporting.

##### Understanding Deferred Revenues

###### Definition

- Deferred revenues are payments received in advance for goods or services to be delivered in the future.
    
- Common examples include rent, magazine subscriptions, customer deposits, and gift cards.
    

###### Original Transaction

- Cash is received and recorded as a liability.
    
- Revenue is recognized only when the service is performed or the product is delivered.
    

###### Reasons for Adjustment

- A portion of the deferred revenue liability is extinguished when the performance obligation is satisfied.
    
- Revenue is then recognized, impacting financial statements.
    

###### Accounts Before Adjustment

- Revenues and net income may be understated.
    
- Liabilities may be overstated, affecting shareholders’ equity.
    

###### Adjusting Entry

- Debit Deferred Revenue to decrease the liability.
    
- Credit Revenue to recognize earned income.
    

###### Accounting Treatment

- Prepayments are recorded as either prepaid expenses or deferred revenues based on the nature of the transaction.
    
- Prepaid expenses are recorded as assets, while deferred revenues are recorded as liabilities.
    

###### Helpful Hint

- The party making the advance payment records a prepaid expense.
    
- The party receiving the payment records a deferred revenue.
    

###### Adjusting Entries Example

- Central Shopping Ltd. rents a store to Joseph’s Bakery Ltd. for $1,200 per month, with the first and last month’s rent paid in advance.
    
- Adjusting entries are required monthly to reflect earned revenue and reduce deferred revenue.

###### Accruals Overview

- Adjusting entries for accruals record expenses incurred or revenues earned not yet recorded.
    
- Accruals differ from prepayments as they are not recognized through transaction journal entries.
    
- Cash for prepayments is paid or received during the accounting period; cash for accruals is paid or received after.
    

###### Accrued Expenses

- Accrued expenses are incurred but not yet paid or recorded.
    
- Common examples include interest, salaries, and income tax.
    
- Adjusting entries are required to:
    
    - Record obligations at the end of the period.
        
    - Recognize expenses incurred during the current accounting period.
        
- Until adjustments are made, liabilities and expenses are understated, leading to overstated net income, retained earnings, and shareholders’ equity.
    
- Adjusting entry results in:
    
    - Increase (debit) to an expense account.
        
    - Increase (credit) to a liability (payable) account.
        

##### Accrued Expenses: Accrued Interest Overview

###### Loan Details

- Sierra Corporation signed a bank loan for $5,000 on October 1.
    
- Interest and principal are repayable in three months.
    
- Interest rate is 6% per annum.
    

###### Factors Determining Interest Incurred

- Principal amount: $5,000
    
- Interest rate: 6% per annum
    
- Loan duration: three months
    

###### Helpful Hint

- Interest is typically calculated using days, but this chapter simplifies it using months.
    

###### Interest Calculation Formula

- Formula: Principal amount × Annual interest rate × Time (in years) = Interest
    
- For Sierra: $5,000 × 6% × (1 month / 12 months) = $25
    

###### Accrual of Interest Expense on October 31

- Adjustment: $25 of interest is owed for the month.
    
- Due date for repayment: January 1.
    

###### Steps for Recording Interest Expense

- **Basic Analysis:** Increase Interest Payable by $25; increase Interest Expense by $25.
    
- **Equation Analysis:** Assets = Liabilities + Shareholders’ Equity; Interest Payable +$25, Interest Expense -$25.
    
- **Debit-Credit Analysis:** Debit Interest Expense $25; Credit Interest Payable $25.
    
- **Journal Entry:**
    
    - Date: October 31
        
    - Debit: Interest Expense $25
        
    - Credit: Interest Payable $25
        
    - Description: To accrue interest on bank loan payable.
        
- **Posting:** Journal entry posted to Interest Expense and Interest Payable T-accounts.

## Accrued Revenues

- Accrued revenues are earned but not yet recorded.
    
- Common examples include interest income.
    
- Adjusting entries for accrued revenues are necessary for:
    
    - Recording the receivable at the end of the period.

- Adjusting entries result in:
    
    - Debit (increase) to an asset account (receivable).

##### Adjustment for Unbilled Advertising Services at Sierra Corporation

###### Overview

- Sierra Corporation earned $200 for advertising services in October.
    
- Services were not billed to clients before October 31.
    
- Revenue needs to be recorded as it has been earned.
    

###### Financial Impact

- Accounts Receivable shows $11,200 owed by clients as of October 31.
    
- Total Service Revenue for the month is $20,600.
    
- If the adjusting entry is not made, the following will be understated:
    
    - Revenues (Service Revenue)
        
    - Net income on the statement of income
        
    - Assets (Accounts Receivable)
        
    - Shareholders’ equity (Retained Earnings)
        

###### Adjustment Process

- Adjustment on October 31: $200 of earned but unbilled advertising service revenue.
    
- Five steps in the adjustment process:
    
    - Basic Analysis
        
    - Equation Analysis
        
    - Debit-Credit Analysis
        
    - Journal Entry
        
    - Posting
        

###### Basic Analysis

- Increase Accounts Receivable by $200 for earned revenue.
    
- Increase Service Revenue by $200, which increases Shareholders’ Equity by $200.
    

###### Equation Analysis

- Accounting equation: Assets = Liabilities + Shareholders’ Equity.
    
- Accounts Receivable: +$200 under Assets.
    
- Service Revenue: +$200 under Retained Earnings of Shareholders’ Equity.
    
- No items under Liabilities.
    

###### Debit-Credit Analysis

- Debit Accounts Receivable: $200 (increases assets).
    
- Credit Service Revenue: $200 (increases revenue).
    

###### Journal Entry

- Date: October 31
    
- Debit: Accounts Receivable $200
    
- Credit: Service Revenue $200
    
- Description: To accrue revenue earned but not billed or collected.
    

###### Posting

- Accounts Receivable T-account:
    
    - Beginning balance (October 15): $20,000 (debit).
        
    - Adjustment (October 31): +$200.
        
    - Closing balance (October 31): $11,200.
        
- Service Revenue T-account:
    
    - Beginning balance (October 15): $20,000 (credit).
        
    - Adjustment (October 31): +$200.
        
    - Closing balance (October 31): $20,600.
        

##### Accrued Revenue and Its Impact on Accounting

###### Overview

- An accrued revenue for one company is an accrued expense for another.
    
- Example: Sierra Corporation has a $200 accrual of service revenue, which is an expense for the client receiving the service.
    

###### Cash Collection Process

- In the next accounting period, cash will be collected for services performed in October and November.
    
- When cash is collected, prior accruals must be considered to avoid double counting revenue.
    
- Example: On November 5, $2,500 cash is received, which includes:
    
    - $200 for October services
        
    - $2,300 for November services
        

###### Journal Entry for Cash Collection

- On November 5, the following entry is recorded:
    
    - Debit Cash: $2,500
        
    - Credit Accounts Receivable: $200
        
    - Credit Service Revenue: $2,300
        
- This entry reflects the collection of accounts and cash receipts from services performed.
    

###### Impact on Accounting Equation

- Assets increase by $2,500 (Cash).
    
- Liabilities remain unchanged.
    
- Shareholders' equity increases by $2,300 (Service Revenue) and decreases by $200 (Accounts Receivable).
    
- Net cash flow increases by $2,500.
