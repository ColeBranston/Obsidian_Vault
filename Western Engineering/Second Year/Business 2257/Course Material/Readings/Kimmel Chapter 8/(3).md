##### Managing Receivables

###### Learning Objective

- Apply the principles of sound accounts receivable management.
    

###### Key Steps in Managing Accounts Receivable

- Determine whom to extend credit to.
    
- Establish a payment period.
    
- Monitor collections.
    
- Evaluate the liquidity of receivables.
    

###### Extending Credit

- Assessing creditworthiness is crucial to avoid revenue loss or risky credit extensions.
    
- Examples of credit policies:
    
    - NFI Group extends credit to public transit authorities but requires payment on delivery for private fleet operators.
        
    - Bombardier Inc. minimizes credit risk through liens, cash deposits, or letters of credit.
        
- Steps to minimize losses:
    
    - Require letters of credit or bank guarantees from risky customers.
        
    - Ask for references from banks and suppliers to assess new customers.
        
    - Utilize credit scoring models for evaluating customer risk.
        
    - Consult credit opinion services like Equifax and TransUnion.
        

###### Establishing a Payment Period

- Determine and communicate the credit term period to customers.
    
- Align credit periods with competitors to avoid losing sales.
    
- Consider offering sales discounts for early payments.
    
- Add interest charges on overdue receivables to encourage timely payment.
    

##### Monitoring Collections

- An accounts receivable aging schedule should be prepared and reviewed monthly.
    
- Accounting software can generate aged listings to assist in estimating the allowance for doubtful accounts.
    
- The aging schedule aids in forecasting future cash inflows for cash budgeting.
    
- It provides insights into the company’s collection experience and identifies problem accounts.
    
- NFI Group Inc. categorizes accounts receivable into:
    
    - Under 60 days past due
        
    - More than 60 days overdue
        
- This categorization helps assess changes in the company’s collection ability.
    
- Credit risk may rise during economic downturns, necessitating ongoing monitoring of credit policies and collection experiences.
    

###### Evaluating Liquidity of Receivables

- Investors and managers monitor the relationship between sales, accounts receivable, and cash collections.
    
- Increased sales typically lead to increased accounts receivable.
    
- If accounts receivable grow faster than sales, it may indicate collection issues.
    
- Loosening credit policies can result in receivables that are hard to collect, impacting liquidity.
    
- Liquidity is defined by how quickly assets can be converted to cash.
    
- The receivables turnover ratio measures how often receivables are collected in a year:
    
    - Calculated by dividing credit sales by average gross accounts receivable.
        
    - Average gross accounts receivable is determined by averaging beginning and ending balances.
        
- In the absence of credit sales data, total sales can be used for calculations.

##### Analysis of NFI Group and Grande West Transportation Receivables

###### Receivables Turnover

- NFI Group's receivables turnover in 2018: 6.5 times (up from 6.2 in 2017).
    
- Grande West Transportation's receivables turnover in 2018: 5.1 times (down from 7.3 in 2017).
    
- Industry average receivables turnover in 2018: 9.1 times (down from 12.6 in 2017).
    
- NFI Group's turnover was higher than Grande West's but lower than the industry average.
    
- Positive trend: NFI Group's turnover increased while Grande West and industry peers decreased.
    

###### Average Collection Period

- NFI Group's average collection period in 2018: 56 days.
    
- Grande West's average collection period in 2018: 72 days (up from 50 days in 2017).
    
- Industry average collection period in 2018: 40 days (up from 29 days in 2017).
    
- NFI Group's collection period is better than Grande West's but worse than the industry average.
    
- Both NFI Group and Grande West improved their ratios in 2018, while industry averages worsened.
    

###### Credit Terms and Collectibility

- Understanding credit terms is essential for assessing receivables ratios.
    
- If NFI Group's credit terms are n/60, its ratios are acceptable; if n/30, there may be collectibility concerns.
    
- Higher receivables turnover and lower average collection period indicate better liquidity.
    

###### Liquidity Assessment

- Receivables turnover and average collection period are key components of liquidity.
    
- These metrics should be analyzed alongside current ratio and inventory turnover.
    
- High receivables and inventory balances can distort the current ratio if not adjusted for declines.
    

###### Decision Tool

- Compare average collection period with credit policies and terms.
    
- Exceeding credit term period indicates potential collectibility issues.
    
- Increased average collection period may reflect declining customer financial health.
    

###### Considerations for Interpretation

- Receivables turnover and collection periods can be misleading in certain retail contexts.
    
- Retail chains with their own credit cards may benefit from slow customer payments through interest income.
    
- Understanding a company's receivables management is crucial for accurate interpretation of these ratios.
    

##### Understanding Cash Flow Management

###### Key Issues in Cash Flow

- Strong sales and net income do not guarantee liquidity.
    
- Poor receivables management can lead to cash shortages.
    
- Example: A consulting business with $18,000 in revenue collected only $2,000 in cash.
    
- Immediate liabilities, such as payroll, can create urgent cash flow problems.
    
- Net income does not equate to cash flow; businesses must manage liquidity effectively.
    

###### Case Study: Harbour Supply Ltd. (HSL)

- HSL sells marine equipment to commercial fishermen in Eastern Canada.
    
- Offers credit with n/30 terms, but struggles with collection times.
    
- Financial data for 2021, 2020, and 2019 shows increasing sales but fluctuating accounts receivable.
    
- Competitors have an average collection period of 41 days.
    

###### Action Plan for Improving Receivables Management

- Calculate receivables turnover and average collection period ratios.
    
- Compare these ratios over the years and against industry averages.
    
- Assess credit extension policies to determine suitable customers.
    
- Establish clear payment periods to encourage timely payments.
    
- Implement monitoring systems for collections to track overdue accounts.
    
- Consider additional strategies to enhance cash flow and reduce collection times.
