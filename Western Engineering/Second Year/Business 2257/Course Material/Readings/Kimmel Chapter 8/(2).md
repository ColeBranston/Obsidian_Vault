##### Understanding Bad Debt Recovery and Collection Practices

###### Carrying Amounts

- Carrying amount for accounts: $216,500

###### Debt Collection Practices

- Collection agencies often contact family and friends of debtors to locate them.
    
- Some agencies create fake social media profiles to gather information.
    
- A woman successfully sued a debt collector for violating her privacy rights on social media.
    
- The Financial Consumer Agency of Canada prohibits contacting friends, family, or employers for debt collection.
    

###### Collecting Uncollectible Accounts

- Companies may continue collection efforts even after an account is written off.
    
- Customers may wish to pay off written-off accounts due to changed circumstances.
    
- This process is known as bad debt recovery.
    

###### Recording Bad Debt Recovery

- Two entries are required for recording bad debt recovery:
    
    - Reverse the write-off to reinstate the customer’s account.
        
    - Record the cash collection from the customer.
        
- Example: T. Ebbet pays $2,500 that was previously written off.
    

###### Journal Entries for Recovery

- First Entry (Reversing Write-off):
    
    - Debit Accounts Receivable: $2,500
        
    - Credit Allowance for Doubtful Accounts: $2,500
        
- Second Entry (Recording Collection):
    
    - Debit Cash: $2,500
        
    - Credit Accounts Receivable: $2,500
        

###### Impact on Financial Statements

- Recovery affects only statement of financial position accounts.
    
- Net effect: Increase in Cash and Allowance for Doubtful Accounts by $2,500.
    
- Accounts Receivable is debited and credited separately for clarity and future credit considerations.

##### Ending Balance of Notes Receivable

###### Overview of Notes Receivable

- Formal credit instrument known as a promissory note.
    
- Written promise to pay a specified amount on demand or at a fixed date.
    
- Used for lending/borrowing money, exceeding transaction limits, or settling accounts receivable.
    

###### Parties Involved

- Maker: The party making the promise to pay.
    
- Payee: The party who will be paid.
    
- Example: Devon Inc. (maker) borrows from Scotiabank (payee).
    

###### Classification

- Note Receivable: Asset for the payee.
    
- Note Payable: Liability for the maker.
    

###### Details of a Promissory Note

- Includes names of maker and payee, principal amount, loan period, interest rate.
    
- Specifies interest payment terms and collateral details.
    

###### Comparison with Accounts Receivable

- Both are financial assets and credit instruments.
    
- Notes Receivable: Formal, interest-bearing from issuance.
    
- Accounts Receivable: Informal, interest-bearing only after due date.
    
- Classification: Accounts receivable are usually current assets; notes can be current or non-current.
    

###### Accounting for Notes Receivable

- Recording notes receivable, estimating uncollectible notes, collecting notes.
    

###### Example Transaction

- On May 1, Tabusintac Inc. accepts a $10,000 note from Raja Ltd. in settlement of an account receivable.
    
- Note has a 6% interest rate, due in four months (September 1).
    
- Journal entry:
    
    - Debit Notes Receivable: $10,000
        
    - Credit Accounts Receivable: $10,000
        

###### Impact on Financial Statements

- Assets increase by $10,000 (Notes Receivable).
    
- Accounts Receivable decrease by $10,000.
    
- No effect on cash flows at the time of the transaction.
    

##### May 1 Notes Receivable Transaction Overview

- Recorded acceptance of Raja's four-month, 6% note.
    
- Notes Receivable: $10,000
    
- Accounts Receivable: $10,000
    
- No interest income recorded at acceptance; interest is earned over time.
    

###### Interest Income Calculation

- Interest is calculated as: Principal x Interest Rate.
    
- Annual interest rate is adjusted for the fraction of the year the note is outstanding.
    
- For Raja's note: Total interest = $200 for four months.
    
- Adjusting entry for May 31:
    
    - Debit Interest Receivable: $50
        
    - Credit Interest Income: $50
        

###### Interest Accrual Entries

- Raja makes identical entries on June 30, July 31, and August 31.
    
- On September 1, collection entry:
    
    - Debit Cash: $10,200
        
    - Credit Notes Receivable: $10,000
        
    - Credit Interest Receivable: $200
        

###### Uncollectible Notes Receivable

- Reported at carrying amount (balance in Notes Receivable minus allowance account).
    
- Individual analysis of each note is preferred for estimating uncollectible amounts.
    
- Bad debts expense and allowance for doubtful notes recorded if collection is doubtful.
    

###### Derecognizing Notes Receivable

- Principal and accrued interest are collected and removed from books.
    
- Notes collected when due are considered honoured.
    
- Defaulting notes require appropriate adjustments, known as dishonoured notes.
    

##### Honoured and Dishonoured Notes Receivable Overview

###### Honoured Notes Receivable

- A note receivable is considered honoured when collected in full at maturity.
    
- Example Entry for Collection on September 1:
    
    - Debit Cash: $10,200
        
    - Credit Notes Receivable: $10,000
        
    - Credit Interest Receivable: $200
        
- This entry reflects the collection of both the principal and accrued interest.
    
- No additional interest income is recorded at maturity if prior adjustments were made.
    

###### Dishonoured Notes Receivable

- A dishonoured note is one that is not paid in full at maturity.
    
- The payee retains a claim against the maker for the principal and unpaid interest.
    
- Example Entry for Dishonoured Note on September 1 (expected collection):
    
    - Debit Accounts Receivable: $10,200
        
    - Credit Notes Receivable: $10,000
        
    - Credit Interest Receivable: $200
        
- If collection is deemed hopeless, the following entry is made:
    
    - Debit Bad Debts Expense: $10,200
        
    - Credit Notes Receivable: $10,000
        
    - Credit Interest Receivable: $200
        

###### Allowance Method for Notes Receivable

- Used when a company has a significant amount of notes receivable.
    
- Management estimates expected credit losses at the end of each accounting period.
    
- An allowance for doubtful notes is established, and bad debts expense is recorded.
    
- When specific notes are written off, the allowance is debited, and notes receivable is credited.
    

###### Example: Sampson Stores Ltd. and Shiraz Corp.

- Sampson accepts a 3-month, 6%, $6,800 note from Shiraz on May 1.
    
- Interest is due at maturity, and adjusting entries are recorded annually.
    
- Assuming Shiraz pays on August 1, the following entries are made:
    
    - Record interest accrued for May and June.
        
    - Record collection of the note and interest on August 1.
        
- If Shiraz cannot pay but collection is expected:
    
    - Debit Accounts Receivable for the total amount.
        
- If Shiraz cannot pay and collection is not expected:
    
    - Debit Bad Debts Expense for the total amount.
