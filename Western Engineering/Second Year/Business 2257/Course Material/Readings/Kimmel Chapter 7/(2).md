
##### Cash Controls Overview

Cash is highly desirable and susceptible to theft, making effective control activities essential for safeguarding cash and ensuring accurate accounting records. This overview discusses the nature of cash, its forms, and control activities related to cash receipts and payments.

###### Definition of Cash

- Cash includes coins, currency (paper money), cheques, and money orders.
    
- Cash can be held on hand or deposited in financial institutions.
    
- Cash does not include:
    
    - Postdated cheques (payable in the future)
        
    - Stale-dated cheques (over six months old)
        
    - Returned cheques (insufficient funds)
        

###### Historical Context of Cash

- First metal coins date back to 610–560 BC.
    
- Paper money appeared in 1685.
    
- Debit and credit cards emerged in the 1970s.
    
- Canada replaced the $1 bill with the “loonie” in 1987 and introduced the “toonie” in 1996.
    
- The Royal Canadian Mint ceased penny production in 2012.
    
- Direct deposits replaced cheques in Canada as of April 1, 2016.
    

###### Emerging Payment Methods

- Digital currencies like PayPal and Bitcoin are gaining popularity.
    
- Apple Pay allows payments via Apple devices.
    
- Contactless payment options are widely used, with 29% of store purchases in Canada being contactless as of mid-2017.
    
- Cash transactions decreased from 40% in 2012 to 30% in 2017.
    

###### Control Activities over Cash Receipts

- Cash receipts can take various forms:
    
    - Over-the-counter receipts
        
    - Electronic receipts
        
    - Cheque receipts (received at sale or by mail)
        
- Internal control procedures vary by company and business type.
    
- Typical controls are illustrated in service or merchandising businesses.
    

##### Understanding Over-the-Counter Receipts

- **Payment Methods:** Retail businesses typically accept cash, credit cards, and debit cards.
    
- **Cash Register Operations:**
    
    - Staff are assigned a float for making change (physical control).
        
    - Sales are recorded using point-of-sale (POS) software, which updates inventory if using a perpetual system.
        
    - Sales can often be recorded by scanning barcodes.
        
- **End of Shift Procedures:**
    
    - Staff must reconcile cash in the register with the float and recorded sales.
        
    - Supervisors often double-check this reconciliation (review and reconciliation).
        
    - Access to the POS system should be restricted to prevent manipulation of sales data (physical control).
        
    - Receipts for debit and credit card sales must be retained and matched with recorded sales.
        
- **Theft Detection:**
    
    - Theft may go undetected if cash is stolen without recording the sale.
        
    - Inventory counts can reveal discrepancies (review and reconciliation).
        
    - Frequency of inventory counts varies by type and cost of inventory.
        
    - Security cameras may be used to monitor sales recording.
        
- **Challenges Without POS Technology:**
    
    - Increased risk of errors when manually entering sales prices.
        
    - Potential for intentional underpricing for friends.
        
    - Inventory discrepancies may not be discovered until later counts.
        
- **Effective Internal Controls:**
    
    - Daily deposits of cash receipts into the bank account are recommended.
        
    - Deposits should be made by authorized personnel, such as the head cashier or general manager.
        
    - Encouraging electronic funds transfers can enhance cash control.
        

##### Control Activities over Cash Receipts

###### Electronic Receipts

- Electronic Funds Transfer (EFT) allows money transfer between bank accounts.
    
- Examples include debit card, credit card transactions, and online banking payments.
    
- Cash is instantly transferred from the customer's account to the company's account.
    
- EFT enhances internal control by minimizing cash handling by employees.
    
- Risks of recording errors and fraud still exist without proper controls.
    

###### Cheque Receipts

- Cheques are still used in business-to-business transactions despite declining popularity.
    
- Cheques received at sale time are recorded in the cash register.
    
- Cheques received by mail come with remittance advice for proper recording.
    
- Segregation of duties is crucial: different employees handle cash and record receipts.
    
- Deposits should be made by employees without record-keeping responsibilities.
    
- Daily comparisons of deposit slips and recorded cash receipts ensure accuracy.
    

###### Control Activities Applications

- **Assignment of Responsibility:** Designated personnel authorized to handle cash receipts.
    
- **Segregation of Duties:** Different individuals for recording cash receipts and handling cash.
    
- **Documentation:** Use remittance advices, cash register tapes, POS reports, and deposit slips.
    
- **Physical Controls:** Store cash in safes, use cash registers, and deposit cash daily.
    
- **Review and Reconciliation:** Supervisors count cash receipts; accountants compare receipts with deposits.
    

##### Control Activities over Cash Receipts at Hamburger Heaven Restaurant

###### Overview

- Six employees share two cash registers.
    
- Owner prefers one person per register but allows teamwork during busy times.
    
- Prices are preprogrammed; daily sales reports are generated.
    
- Cash is counted and compared to sales reports by one employee.
    
- Cash is stored in an unlocked office drawer.
    

###### Violations of Control Activities

- **Assignment of Responsibility:** Multiple employees use the same register, making it hard to identify responsibility for cash shortages.
    
- **Segregation of Duties:** Employees who handle cash also verify receipts, increasing the risk of theft.
    
- **Review and Reconciliation:** Cash counts should be verified by a second employee or the owner for accuracy.
    
- **Physical Controls:** Cash should be deposited promptly or secured in a safe or locked drawer.
    

###### Action Plan

- Implement clear assignment of responsibility for cash registers.
    
- Ensure segregation of duties between cash handling and cash verification.
    
- Establish a review process involving a second employee for cash counts.
    
- Enhance physical controls by securing cash in a safe or making timely bank deposits.
    

##### Control Activities over Cash Payments

###### Payment Methods

- Cash payments are used for various expenses, liabilities, and asset purchases.
    
- Cheque and electronic funds transfer (EFT) are preferred over cash for control effectiveness.
    
- Petty cash funds are used for minor payments not suitable for cheques.
    

###### Accounting Matters

- In 2017, EFT transactions were the most common payment method for Canadian businesses.
    
- 80% of business transactions were made by EFT, totaling over $4.2 trillion.
    
- Businesses wrote approximately 510 million cheques worth about $3.9 trillion.
    
- Cheques are commonly used for taxes, rent, services, and payroll payments.
    
- Bank reconciliation remains a crucial internal control over cash.
    

##### Control Activities over Cash Payments

###### Overview of Good Control for Cheques

- Cheques should be signed by at least two authorized employees.
    
- Cheque signers must review supporting documentation before signing.
    
- Clear segregation of duties between cheque signing (authorization) and accounts payable (recording).
    
- Pre-numbered cheques must be used and accounted for in the payment process.
    
- Cheques should never be pre-signed.
    
- Restricted access to blank cheques, digital signature software, and cheque-signing machines is essential.
    

###### Electronic Payments

- Payments can be made electronically, such as direct deposits for employee salaries.
    
- Proper assignment of responsibility and segregation of duties enhances internal control with electronic funds transfers (EFT).
    

###### Control Activities Applied to Cash Payments

- **Assignment of Responsibility:** Authorize designated personnel to sign cheques or approve electronic payments.
    
- **Segregation of Duties:** Different individuals should approve and record payments; the signer should not record the payment.
    
- **Documentation:** Use prenumbered cheques and ensure each cheque has an approved invoice.
    
- **Physical Controls:** Store cash in safes with limited access; restrict access to blank cheques and cheque-signing machines; use electronic payments when possible.
    
- **Review and Reconciliation:** Compare cheques with invoices and reconcile the bank statement monthly.
    

###### Cash Management and Data Analytics

- Intelligent cash drawers automatically record cash transactions and provide real-time reports.
    
- Sensors weigh cash denominations to calculate the number of bills or coins.
    
- Automation reduces time spent counting cash at the end of shifts.
    
- Records all cash movements and alerts management for discrepancies.
    
- Generates alerts for excessive cash or when adjustments are needed.
    
- Reports on error rates by cashier and frequency of refund transactions.

- **Segregation of Duties:**
    
    - Staff can authorize cash payments and have custody of cash, violating this control.
        
- **Documentation:**
    
    - Cash payments eliminate payment records, unlike cheques or electronic payments.
        
- **Physical Controls:**
    
    - Cheques are stored in an unlocked drawer, which should be secured.

###### Understanding Debits and Credits

- Debits are amounts deducted from the account (e.g., cheques).
    
- Credits are amounts added to the account (e.g., deposits).
    
- Bank's perspective: Funds held are liabilities, increasing with credits and decreasing with debits.
    

##### Understanding Bank Credits and Debits

###### Overview

- Relationship between bank credits and company debits.
    
- Bank accounts viewed as liabilities.
    
- Impact of deposits and withdrawals on bank liabilities.
    

###### Bank's Perspective

- Your account is a liability to the bank.
    
- Depositing funds increases the bank's liability.
    
- Withdrawing funds decreases the bank's liability.
    

###### Transaction Effects

- Deposits result in a credit to your account.
    
- Withdrawals result in a debit to your account.
    

##### Amounts Deducted from a Bank Account (Debits)

###### Types of Deductions

- **Cheques:** Written orders signed by authorized employees to pay a specific sum to a designated recipient.
    
- **EFT Payments:** Electronic funds transfers for various payments, including salaries and recurring bills.
    
- **Other Payments:** Pre-authorized payments for loans, rent, and insurance.

###### NSF Cheques

- NSF (non-sufficient funds) cheques are returned by the bank due to insufficient funds.
    
- Example: Returned cheque—NSF for $425.60 (April 21).
    
- Previously deposited cheques must be reversed, reducing the bank account balance.
    
- The bank returns the NSF cheque to the depositor as notification.
    

###### Impact of NSF Cheques

- The company informs the customer of the declined payment.
    
- Bank charges a service fee for processing returned cheques.
    
- Example: Laird incurred a $40 NSF service charge (April 21).
    
- Creates an account receivable for the original amount plus service charge.
    
- Reduces cash in the depositor’s bank account.

###### Reconciling the Bank Account

- Bank and company records often differ due to:
    
    - Timing differences in transaction recording.
        
    - Errors made by either party.
        
- Common timing differences include:
    
    - Outstanding cheques: Cheques mailed but not yet cleared.
        
    - Deposits in transit: Deposits made but not yet recorded by the bank.
        

###### Errors in Recording

- Errors can occur due to:
    
    - Incorrect recording of amounts (e.g., $450 recorded as $45).
        
    - Bank charging a cheque to the wrong account.
        
    - Dependence on correct account information for direct deposits and EFTs.
        
- Frequency of errors is influenced by the effectiveness of internal controls.
    

##### Bank Errors and Reconciliation Procedures

###### Bank Errors

- Bank errors, though less frequent than company errors, can occur.
    
- Example: Scotiabank mistakenly deposited $171 million into a doctor's account, taking four months to correct.
    
- Common bank errors include misplaced debits, but rarely involve large sums.
    

###### Reconciliation Procedures

- Bank reconciliations should be prepared by an employee with no cash-related responsibilities (segregation of duties).
    
- Without segregation, embezzlement may go unnoticed, as employees can misstate amounts to hide theft.
    
- Reconciliation involves comparing the bank statement balance with the company’s book balance.
    
- Reconciling items must be identified to determine the reconciled cash balance.
    
- Reconciliation is typically performed monthly, starting with the ending cash balances from both the bank statement and the company’s books.
    

###### Steps in Bank Reconciliation

- **Per Bank Statement:**
    
    - Adjustments to the bank balance:
        
        - Deposits in transit (add)
            
        - Outstanding checks (subtract)
            
        - Bank errors (adjust)
            
- **Per Company’s Books:**
    
    - Adjustments to the book balance:
        
        - EFT collections, interest, and other deposits (add)
            
        - EFT payments, service charges, and NSF checks (subtract)
            
        - Company errors (adjust)
            

###### Conclusion

- Both bank and company errors must be addressed to ensure accurate financial records.
    
- Regular reconciliations help detect discrepancies and prevent fraud.
    

##### Reconciling Items to Cash Balance per Bank

The cash balance per the bank statement must be adjusted by the following items:

- Deposits in transit
    
- Outstanding cheques
    
- Bank errors (if any)
    

After factoring in these reconciling items, the reconciled cash balance reflects what the bank balance would have been if all items were accounted for.

###### Deposits in Transit (+)

To determine deposits in transit:

- Compare deposits on the bank statement with:
    
    - Deposits in transit from the previous reconciliation
        
    - Deposits recorded in the company’s books
        
- Deposits in transit are recorded in the company’s books but not yet by the bank.
    
- Once processed, these deposits will increase the company’s account balance.
    
- Check if prior period deposits in transit have cleared.
    

Calculation for deposits in transit at the end of the period:

- Deposits in transit at beginning of period
    
- + Deposits recorded in company’s books this period
    
- - Deposits recorded on this period’s bank statement
    
- = Deposits in transit at end of period
    

###### Outstanding Cheques (−)

To determine outstanding cheques:

- Compare paid cheques on the bank statement with:
    
    - Cheques outstanding from the previous reconciliation
        
    - Cheques issued by the company
        
- Outstanding cheques are recorded in the company’s books but have not cleared the bank.
    
- These cheques must be deducted from the balance per bank in the reconciliation process.
    
- Outstanding cheques from prior periods that have cleared will not be listed as reconciling items.
    

Calculation for outstanding cheques at the end of the period:

- Outstanding cheques at beginning of period
    
- + Cheques recorded in company’s books this period
    
- - Cheques recorded on this period’s bank statement
    
- = Outstanding cheques at end of period
    

##### Outstanding Cheques and Bank Reconciliation Overview

###### Outstanding Cheques Calculation

- Outstanding cheques at beginning of period
    
- Plus: Cheques recorded in company’s books this period
    
- Minus: Cheques recorded on this period’s bank statement
    
- Equals: Outstanding cheques at end of period
    

###### Bank Errors

- Identify any errors made by the bank.
    
- Example: If a deposit of $1,693 is recorded as $1,639, add the difference of $54 to the bank balance.
    
- Determine if the error should be added or subtracted based on the hypothetical bank balance without the error.
    

###### Reconciling Items to Cash Balance per Company’s Books

- Reconciling items relate to amounts not yet recorded in the company’s books.
    
- Adjustments are made upon receiving the bank statement.
    

###### EFT Collections and Deposits

- Electronic funds transfers and interest earned should be added to the balance per books.
    
- Compare bank statement deposits with company records for unrecorded amounts.
    

###### EFT Payments and Deductions

- Unrecorded payments, service charges, and NSF cheques should be deducted from the balance per books.
    
- Record any service charges not previously noted in company records.
    

###### Book Errors

- Identify errors made by the company.
    
- Example: If a cheque is recorded incorrectly, adjust the balance per books accordingly.
    
- Determine if the error should be added or subtracted based on the hypothetical book balance without the error.
