##### Understanding Cash Management and Reporting

###### Importance of Cash Management

- Critical for paying bills and purchasing goods.
    
- Cash is an unproductive asset unless invested.
    
- Management must know current cash levels and future cash needs.
    

###### Reporting Cash

- Reported in two financial statements:
    
    - Statement of Financial Position: Cash available at a specific time.
        
    - Statement of Cash Flows: Receipts and payments of cash over a period.
        
- Ending cash amount on the statement of cash flows matches the cash amount on the statement of financial position.
    
- Cash is the most liquid asset, listed first in current assets.
    

###### Cash Equivalents

- Short-term, highly liquid investments for meeting cash needs.
    
- Must be easily convertible to cash with minimal risk of value change.
    
- Examples include:
    
    - Treasury bills (T-bills)
        
    - Commercial paper
        
    - Money market funds
        
    - Short-term bank deposits
        

###### Bank Overdrafts

- Occurs when a company writes cheques exceeding its cash balance.
    
- Pre-approved amounts are known as operating lines of credit.
    
- Overdrafts are reported as current liabilities (bank indebtedness).
    
- Bank overdrafts are deducted from cash equivalents if they are part of cash management practices.
    

###### Conclusion

- Cash management is essential for operational efficiency.
    
- Understanding cash reporting helps in effective financial planning.
    
- Proper management of cash and cash equivalents ensures liquidity and financial stability.
    

##### Understanding Cash Equivalents and Cash Management

###### Cash Equivalents

- Liquid assets with insignificant risk convertible into a known amount of cash.
    
- Includes cash items such as:
    
    - Coins
        
    - Currency (paper money)
        
    - Cheques
        
    - Money orders
        
    - Amounts on deposit with banks or financial institutions
        
- Cash equivalents consist of:
    
    - Low-risk debt investments with a maturity of less than 90 days.
        
    - Assets that can be easily converted to cash.
        
- Bank overdrafts arise from using an eligible operating line of credit or credit facility.
    

###### Managing Cash

- Many companies face challenges in cash management despite generating sales.
    
- Example: Martineau Designs Ltd.
    
    - Founded by Erin Martineau after leaving a stable job.
        
    - Faced financial difficulties despite high clothing orders.
        
    - Struggled to collect cash quickly to support operations.
        
- Operating cycle of a clothing manufacturing company:
    
    - Purchases raw materials on credit, creating a liability to suppliers.
        
    - Manufactures clothing, incurring salary obligations to employees.
        
    - Sells clothing to retailers on credit, delaying cash inflow.
        
- Ensuring sufficient cash is a major management challenge.
    

###### Basic Principles of Cash Management

- Maintain adequate cash flow to meet operational needs.
    
- Monitor the timing of cash inflows and outflows.
    
- Implement strategies to accelerate cash collections from customers.
    

# Cash Management Strategies for Erin Martineau

##### Principles of Cash Management

###### Increase the Speed of Collection on Receivables

- Aim to reduce the average collection period from 30 days to 20 days.
    
- Consider the impact on customer relationships when enforcing earlier payment terms.
    
- Offer cash discounts to encourage quicker payments.
    

###### Keep Inventory Levels Low

- Avoid tying up cash in large inventories of raw materials and finished goods.
    
- Minimize storage costs associated with operating and renting warehouse space.
    
- Be aware of the risk of inventory becoming obsolete due to fashion trends or technological changes.
    
- Balance inventory levels to prevent lost sales while conserving cash.
    

###### Take Advantage of Credit Periods

- Track bill due dates to avoid early payments that reduce cash availability.
    
- Utilize the full payment period offered by suppliers without damaging credit ratings.
    

###### Plan the Timing of Major Expenditures

- Consider the operating cycle when planning major expenditures.
    
- Make expenditures during off-seasons when excess cash is available.
    

###### Invest Idle Cash

- Invest excess cash to earn returns, even if only overnight.
    
- Ensure investments are liquid and risk-free to avoid cash crises.
    
- Common options include treasury bills or money market funds for short-term investments.
    

###### Prepare a Cash Budget

- Create a cash budget to forecast cash flows over one to two years.
    
- Identify potential financing needs before they arise.
    
- Determine when excess cash will be available for debt repayment or investments.
