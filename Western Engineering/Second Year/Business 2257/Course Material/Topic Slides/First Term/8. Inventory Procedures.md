
## Inventory Procedures Using T-accounts

- Accounting for discounts, returns and allowances can be very complicated because the entry required depends on your role in the situation

- t is very important that you understand what company you are recording transactions for so that you know if your company is the **BUYER** or the **SELLER**

- Most companies are *both* the **buyer** and the **seller** at different points. You must isolate the specific event in question to determine if the company is the **buyer** or the **seller** for that specific situation.

	- If your company is **purchasing inventory** then it is the role of the **BUYER** that you should be concerned about

	- If your company is **selling goods** then it is the role of the **SELLER** that you should be focused on

- The first step is to determine whether you are the buyer or the seller

- If you determine that your company is the BUYER, the terms associated with the entries are:

	- Purchase discounts
	- Purchase returns & allowance
	- You must credit the Inventory account directly

- The purchase of inventory on account would be recorded as follows:

**Discount terms**: are often given in the following format—3/10, net 30 where:

1.  A 3% discount is granted if payment is received during the discount period,  
2.  the discount period is 10 days and  
3. the account is due in 30 days.

**NOTE:** The discount period can start on the date the goods are shipped or on  
the date the goods are received, depending on negotiations between the  
supplier and the customer. **For the purposes of this course, you will always**  
**be told when the discount period starts.**

**The entry to record a purchase return/allowance is:**

	DR Accounts Payable (AP), or Cash  
		CR Inventory

### Key Points

1. **Record all relevant costs in the Inventory account.**
	- Purchases, duties and transportation-in are debits.
	- Purchase discounts and returns and allowances are credits.

2. **Ignore “net cost”**
	- Initially, you post the entire amount of the purchase, before discount, into the inventory account. Only record discounts when the liability is paid the discount period.

3. **Calculate COGS at the end of the period only.**
	- We only use the periodic inventory, as opposed to perpetual inventory.

4. **Record transportation charges, incurred when delivering goods to a customer, in an expense account (i.e., delivery expense) (as opposed to the costs to transport goods to us from suppliers).**
