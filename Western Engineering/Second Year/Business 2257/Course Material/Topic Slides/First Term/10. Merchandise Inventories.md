
- Merchandise inventory cases can get complicated quickly due to the number of different items you are responsible for keeping track of (duties, shipping terms, credit terms, etc.).

- An excellent way to approach merchandise inventory cases and keep track of all the important items at the same time is through the use of a merchandise inventory checklist.

- A checklist is not a mandatory step in preparing a merchandise inventory case, but it helps you organize your information in a clear way.

- See the next page for a sample of a checklist. In each cell, you can keep track of the different components of each inventory purchase.

![[Merchandise Inventory Checklist]]

### Suggested Approach

1. Read the case carefully. Highlight important items such **as duties, FOB terms, credit**  
**terms, etc.**

2. Go to case exhibits. Understand what is contained in them.

3. Handle each purchase individually and **in sequence** of when ownership took place.
	- Break out all relevant costs individually.
	- Keep track of the net units per purchase in its own T-account.
	- Calculate the unit cost of the purchase.

4. Determine the cost of goods available for sale (COGAFS) and units available for sale (UAFS).

5. Calculate the value of the ending inventory using one of the three methods available:
	- **Specific identification**
	- **First-in, first-out (FIFO)**
	- **Average cost**

6. Determine the cost of goods sold (COGS).

### Rounding Rules

1. Place the net purchase price in the Inventory ($) T-account to the nearest dollar (including Ending Balance and COGS)

2. Round all unit costs to **three** decimal places
