### Valuation of the WIP Ending Balance

- One of the most difficult tasks involved in accounting for manufacturing activities is to value the ending balance in WIP

- The formula to value the ending balance in WIP is always: 

pRM + pDL + pFOH

It is difficult to assign all units a common value since they may be at different stages of completion

Instead, managers use an estimate where they break down the  
partially completed goods into three components

- Raw materials
- Direct Labour
- Factory Overhead

#### Components of WIP

**Raw Materials In Partially Completed Goods (pRM)**

Raw materials can be traced to individual units. A company’s production workers or engineers can estimate how much raw material has been used in each unit and then calculate the cost of raw materials in the partially completed goods inventory.

**Direct Labour In Partially Completed Goods (pDL)**

Job timing sheets or standard times can be used to get a reasonable estimate as to how much time has been put into the partially completed goods.

This figure would be multiplied by an **average direct labour cost** to get pDL.

**Note:** You will always be given the information required for pRM and pDL. You are not expected to estimate these numbers on your own.

**Factory Overhead in Partially Completed Goods (pFOH)**

These costs are more difficult to assign to the partially completed goods because they are incurred by the business as a whole (e.g., how much factory rent is in a partially completed product)

Therefore, managers must make an estimate to value pFOH

### Calculating pFOH

In order to allocate factory overhead, managers look for a cost to serve as a “proxy”.

The "proxy" must have three characteristics:

1. Measurable in the partially completed units
2. Considered to be a **cost driver** (i.e., an item that tends to be somewhat proportional to overhead costs
3. Spread evenly throughout the production process

Common proxies include machine hours, direct labor cost/hours, and raw materials. In cases, look for the term “spread evenly” to help you determine the appropriate proxy to use.
