
Lease agreement always involves a lessor and lessee.

**Lessor**: 
	The person providing the asset
**Lessee**: 
	The person making payments to the lease for the use of an asset.

*** in 2257 We will only ever account for the perspective of the lessee ***

Leases are classified as either an **operating lease** or a **finance lease**, account accordingly.

(So far we've been accounting for the operating lease)
### Operating Lease

- Under IFRS, operating leases are defined as a lease with a term under a year.

-> Operating leases don't allow for the lessee to buy the asset during or at the end of the lease agreement. 

**Normal Accounting:**

DR Lease expense
	CR Cash/Accounts Payable/Prepaid lease
### Finance Lease

- Under IFRS, lease agreements longer than a year, and/or contain an option to purchase the asset are considered finance leases.

->  Each payment made from the lessee to the lessor is the combination of the principle itself, and the interest related to the remaining amount on the lease. 

- Each finance lease is considered as an asset with a liability pair:
	Asset: The asset under the lease
	Liability: Lease Obligation

**Transaction:**
	DR Asset under lease
		Cr Lease Obligation

The amount we record is equal to the fair value or present value of the lease payments. We have to use time value of money since finance leases have terms that go beyond 1 year.

$\therefore$ This would be the combination of fair value calculations given from the interest (annuity table), and the principle (top/regular table).

#### Calculating the Fair Value of Lease

1. Take the value of one lease payment.
2. Calculate the number of payment dates.
3. Use the implicit rates as *i* and number of payment dates minus 1 as *n* in the present value of annuities table to find the PV factor.
4. Multiply the PV factor by the value of one lease payment.
5. Add the value of one payment to the amount found in *step 4* (PV of future payments)

**Note:** We use. annuities table because of constant payments over time

**In Bus2257 lease payments will always take place annually, and the first payment will always be when the lease begins.**

$\therefore$ we deduct 1 from the *n* value (number of payments) when calculating present value because the first payment is already equal to the present value.

**Transaction**:
	DR Asset under lease
		CR Lease obligation
