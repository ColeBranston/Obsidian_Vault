#### 2. Units-of-production Method

This method is also referred to as units-of-output, or units-of-activity. 

Depreciation for the period = Depreciation per unit of output x Units used in the period  

Depreciation per unit of output = Historical cost – Estimated residual value  

Estimated units of output in the life of the asset

So, depreciation = ${HC - RV \over Total\;Units} \times Units\;used$

**Note:** Calendar time that an asset is in use does not matter when calculating depreciation using the units-of-production method.

### For All Depreciation Methods

- In the case of the straight-line and the units-of-production methods, do not depreciate beyond the estimated years of useful life. In the case of the diminishing and double-diminishing balance methods, do not depreciate beyond the estimated residual value. 

- Once the asset has been depreciated for its entire useful life, its book value will equal its estimated residual value.  

- Round all depreciation calculations to the nearest half month (for the purpose of this course)

### Accounting for Sales, Retirements and Trade-ins

1. Depreciate the asset up to the point of the sale, retirement or trade-in. 
2. .Calculate the asset’s book value at the point of the sale, retirement or trade-in.
3. Compare the asset’s book value with the cash received or the trade-in allowance granted.  
	- If the BV is greater than the cash received or the trade-in allowance granted, then a **LOSS** has occurred. Losses are recorded as **EXPENSES**.  
	- If the BV is less than the cash received or the trade-in allowance granted, then a **GAIN** has occurred. Gains are recorded as a reduction in **EXPENSES** (contra-expense account)

4. Record the entry :  
	a. Record the effect on Cash.  
	- In the case of a sale—Debit  
	- In the case of a trade-in—Credit  
		- Cash = Purchase Price – Trade-in allowance  
	b. Remove the old asset from the accounting records.  
	- Historical cost  
	- Accumulated depreciation  
	
	c. Record the gain/loss.  
		**Gain or Loss = Cash Received or Trade-in Value – Book Value (HC – A/D)**  

	d. In the case of a trade-in, add the new asset to the accounting records

### Trade-ins of Long-lived Assets

**Recording a Gain on Trade:**

A gain is recorded when the trade-in allowance granted is greater than the book value of the asset.  

Example: A $16,000 truck with accumulated depreciation totaling $12,800 is traded for a new truck. The trade-in allowance was $4,800 and taken off the list price of $20,000 (thus cash paid = $15,200).

### Impairment of a Tangible Asset

 - If the carrying amount of a tangible asset ever exceeds its recoverable amount, the asset is considered to be impaired. 

- Recoverable amount = Fair value less selling costs  

- To record an impairment:  

	**DR** Impairment Loss (expense)  
		**CR** Accumulated Depreciation – Equipment

Impairments can sometimes be reversed.  
- Under IFRS, if an impairment is reversed, the asset can only be returned to its carrying value before impairment.  


- To reverse an impairment:  

	**DR** Accumulated Depreciation – Equipment  
		**CR** Impairment Loss (expense)

### Intangible Assets

- Do not have physical substance, and involve rights, privileges, and/or competitive advantages.  

- Must be identifiable, therefore meeting one of the two following criteria:  

	- It can be separated from the company and sold.  
	- It is based on contractual or legal rights, regardless of whether or not it can be separated from the company.

#### Accounting For Intangible Assets

Recorded at cost.  

- Intangibles include assets such as goodwill, patents, trademarks etc. 
- 2 types:  

	1. Finite (limited) life  
	2. Indefinite (unlimited) life

##### Examples:

1. Finite Lives
	- Patents
	- Copyrights
	- Research and Development Costs

2. Indefinite Lives
	- Trademarks, Trade Names, and Brands
	- Franchises and Licenses

##### (Cont'd)

- For an intangible asset with a finite life, its cost must be allocated over its useful life.  
	- This is called amortization  

- Amortizable amount = cost less residual value  

- Useful life = the shorter of 1) the estimated useful life and 2) the legal life.  

- The amortizable amount would be amortized equally over the length of useful life selected

- For an asset with an indefinite life, we must consider what we call impairment, instead of amortization.  

- Impairment tests must be performed annually on intangible assets under  

- IFRS standards, while under ASPE standards, they are only done when indications of an impairment are present.  

- If any impairment is evident, it must be written down to its recoverable amount. 

- Under IFRS, an impairment can be reversed for all intangible assets except goodwill. 

- Under ASPE, reversals are not permitted.

##### Research & Development Costs

- Research and development is considered when a company develops intangible assets internally rather than acquiring them from another party.  

- During research, costs are incurred but it is not yet known if these costs will have a future benefit.
	- These costs are accounted for as a “research expense”.  

- Once certain criteria is met, expenditures are considered to be development with a known future benefit.  
	- These costs are recorded in the asset account “Development Costs”.

##### Criteria of "Development Costs"

All of the following criteria must be met to indicate that a project being developed will have a future benefit:  
- The project is technically feasible  
- The company has the desire to complete the development  
- The company is able to complete the development  
- A market exists for the product  

Post-development begins when commercial production of the productor process developed begins. It is at this time the development costs commence amortization over the useful life of the product or process developed.
