
**Purpose:** To provide information as to how the activities of the enterprise have been financed and how its financial resources have been used during the period covered by the statement.

**Answers The Questions:**
- Where did our money **come from**?
- Where did we **use it?**

**End Goal**:
- Dissect the cash account
- Reveal the combination of factors causing the changes to the cash account.
- Understand what this means to the company.

>[!important] Cash Basis
>**Three Sections**
>1. Operations
>2. Investing Activities
>3. Financing Activities
>
>*The current proper order is Operation, Investing then Financing. Some cases written in the past will use the order operations, financing then investing.*

## 1. Operations

How much cash did we generate (use) from out regular day-to-day operations? 

**Steps**

1. Record net income.
2. Add back all non-cash expenses
3. Negate the effect of items which do not relate to normal activities such as the loss on sale of a fixed asset. Add back losses. Subtract gains. 
4. Account for the changes in the current accounts associated with normal operations (ie., non-financial)
	- An increase of a current asset is a **USE** of cash
	- A decrease of a current asset is a **SOURCE** of cash.
	- An increase of a current liability us a **SOURCE** of cash.
	- A decrease of a current liability is a **USE** of cash.

**Add SOURCE (+)**
**Subtract USES (-)**

Steps 1-4 provide: **NET CASH FLOW FROM OPERATIONS**

## 2. Investing Activities

How much cash was used (generated) from investments (or divestments)?

**Steps**:
1. Focus on the SOFP and consider the effects on cash of all the changes in the level of investment for **NON-CURRENT** assets (eg. Land, equipment).
2. Reconstruct any depreciable asset accounts considering depreciation and any losses or gains on disposals in order to determine the **CASH** impact of fixed asset transactions.
	- For non-depreciating assets (eg. Land), treat year-over-year changes like any other account. Eg. If the amount increases, show it as a use as a separate line item. 

## 3.  Financing

How much cash was generated through financing activities (debt or equity financing)?

**Steps:**
1. Look at the statement of financial position (SOFP) for any increase or decrease in the level of loans, stock, personal investment.
2. Reconstruct the Retained Earnings account (or Owner's Equity account) to determine the level of dividends (or drawings).
3. Subtract dividends/drawings.

Steps 1-3 provide: **NET SOURCES OF CASH FROM FINANCING**

**Calculating Ending Cash**:

Did the company have a positive or negative cash flow? What was the impact on the company's ending cash balance?

**Steps:**
1. **ADD** net Cash Flow from Operations, Cash Flow from Investments and Cash Flor from Financing to the *opening cash balance* for the year (ie. **LAST** year's ending balance from the SOFP).
2. This total should reconcile with the ending cash balance for **THIS** fiscal year.

# Uses of the SCF

**A SCF cam highlight the answers to the following questions:**
- How much cash did we make (lose) from operations? Is this sustainable?
- What are the largest sources and uses of cash? Does this meet expectations? Is this sustainable?
- Is the company appropriately matching its sources/uses of cash?

**The interpretation must be:**
- Environmentally specific
- Business Specific

Often the SCG generates more questions then answers.
use in conjunction with ration analysis in order to answer the questions posed.

# The Statement of Cash Flows

**Remember:**

A business that is **UNPROFITABLE** can **SURVIVE**, but **INSOLVENCY** (insufficient cash to pay debts) could mean **BUSINESS FAILURE**
