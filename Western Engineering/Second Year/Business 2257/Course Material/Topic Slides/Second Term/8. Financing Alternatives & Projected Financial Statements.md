
**WHY MAKE THEM?**

1. Forecast cash needs
2. Provide information for creditors
3. View the future in dollars and perhaps alter operating policies so that goals may be reached
4. Budget

## Projected Statements

1. Historical Financial performance -- ratios, old statements
2. Management policies and estimates
3. Environmental factors
4. "Gut feel"

## Types of Statements:

1. Pro forma Income Statement
2. Pro forma Statement of Financial Position (Balance Sheet)

## Methodology of Projecting Statements

**Projected Income Statement**

**Process:**
1. Estimate sales forecast
2. Estimate expense levels using vertical analysis, management policy, and/or past income statements
3. Apply tax rate

**Projected Statement of Financial Position (Balance Sheet)**

**Process:**
1. Enter items not expected to change from the last statement of financial position (balance sheet).
2. 
	a) For a corporation: Enter retained earnings:
		= retained earnings last year *plus* projected net income (or subtract projected net loss), *less* any dividends
	b) For a sole in proprietorship: Enter owner's equity:
		= owner's equity (includes retained earnings) last year *plus* projected net income (or subtract projected net loss), *plus* planned additional investment *less* any drawings.

3. Use liquidity and management policy to determine expected level of accounts receivable, accounts payable and inventory.

**Projected A/R = projected credit sales × # of days ÷ 360**  
**Projected Inv. = projected COGS × # of days ÷ 360**  
**Projected A/P = projected credit purchases (or COGS) × # of days ÷ 360**

4. Include planned fixed asset acquisition and include depreciation from income statement. 
5. Include planned debt financing.
6. Estimate accounts not yet filled using historical data.
7. use the cash budget to get cash and/or bank loan balances.
	- **IF** the subtotal of liabilities & equity exceeds the asset subtotal, plug for **CASH** (i.e.. no financing is needed).
	- **IF** the asset subtotal exceeds the liabilities & equity subtotal (ie. more financing is needed), plug for **ADDITIONAL FINANCING** (or **BANK LOAN**).

## Generic Financing Strategies

**IF, according to the projected statement of financial position, additional financing is required (ie. the plug was in the LIABILITIES section), then you must consider the financing options available:**

**INTERNAL FINANCING**:

a) Improve spread between cash revenues and cash expenses
b) Internal financing/working capital manipulation (ie. $\downarrow$ receivables and inventory, $\uparrow$ payables)
c) Divest (sell) fixed assets

**EXTERNAL FINANCING**:

a) Raise Equity
b) Take on Debt
- Short-term financing
- Long-term financing

**When deciding on the form  of financing that will be sought consider:**
- The timing of the cash needs (short-term or long-term)
- The current financial structure
- The likelihood of obtaining the financing

**One the appropriate financing source has been selected, the projected statement of financial position can be finalized**

## Sensitivity

- The art of playing "**WHAT IF**"
- Because estimates are used, they will inevitably be wrong.
- Ask what happens (*for instance*_)
	- Sales decrease by 30%?, increase by 20%?
	- Raw materials increase by 30%?, etc.

**It is impossible to do a sensitivity analysis on every possible scenario. To determine which criteria to perform sensitivity on, use the following guide:**
- The greater the uncertainty, the greater the chance of a "wrong" assumption.
- The greater the impact on the bottom line, the more important it is to know "what if?"

**Therefore, the most common items on which to perform sensitivity**:
- Sales
- Cost of goods sold (ie. purchase)
