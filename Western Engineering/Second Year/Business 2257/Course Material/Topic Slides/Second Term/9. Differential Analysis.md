
- The differential cash flow model is a **SHORT-TERM** decision-making framework used to assess **FUTURE** opportunities in a clear, concise manner. 

- The framework can only be used when analyzing two (or more) quantifiable, short-term alternatives. Many times one alternative is the status quo.

- Long-term decisions require the study of time capital cost of money and tax shields -- *beyond the scope of this course*.

## Decisions Solvable with Differential Analysis

**Examples:**

- Make in-house vs. buy.
- Accept vs. reject special order request
- Lower vs. Raise Price
- Maintain vs. Delete Sub-unit
- Expand vs. Status-quo

### Why Differential Analysis?

**Preferred to Projected Statements Because:**
- Projected statements are crowded with irrelevant information.
- Projected statements are accrual in nature, whereas the important decisions revolve around cash.

**Preferred to Breakeven Analysis Because:**
- Differential analysis is broken down into a ROI figure which can be compared to a standard of risk measurement.
- Comparison of the ROI to the hurdle rate is more straightforward than the evaluation of breakeven as a percentage of market, or margin of safety.

### 3. Differential ROI

$$\text{ROI}={\text{Differential Cash Flow}\over \text{Differential Investment}}$$

Relates to the size of the investment to the recurring cash flows expected.

>[!example] 
>	For Sox R Us, the ROI of the new plan is: 
$${$25,000\over $45,000}\approx 55.6\%$$

### 4. Differential Payback:

An alternate means of expressing the relationship between return and investment. Payback measures the length of time needed before an investment pays for itself. 

$$\text{Payback} = {\text{Differential Investment}\over \text{Differential Cash Flow}}$$

>[!example] 
>Sox R Us, would recoup its investment in:
>$${$45,000\over $25,000} \approx 1.8 \text{ years}$$

- When calculating differential ROI, or payback, do not include financing and related interest costs in the differential analysis.
	- Financing decisions are **NOT** made on a project by project basis. Firms seek to achieve a certain overall debt to equity ratio.
	- Differentials are concerned with **OPERATING DECISIONS**.

- **"Return"** can be loosely defined as the amount of money available to pay the investors. If one of the "investors" is a creditor (such as a bank), then payments are made by way of interest. If the return generated is not sufficient to make these interest payments, then the decision will likely be turned down.

**Once ROI or Payback has been calculated, it should be compared to a company-specific, venture-specific HURLE RATE**

### 5. Hurdle Rate

- The minimum target rate of return for the project specified in the differential analysis.
- Hurdle rates are company-specific expected rates of return based on risk/return analysis.
- Most plans will make money. However, since capital resources are scarce, firms must choose between various money-making plans. Thus, the return must clear the hurdle rate.
- Hurdle rates are always higher than the bank rate since they contain a risk premium factor. 

>[!example]  Hurdle Rate
>Sox R Us uses a hurdle rate of 33% when assessing new opportunities.  
>Therefore, quantitatively, the new plan looks good. 
>- ROI (55.6%) > Hurdle Rate (33%)

## Time Value of Money & NPV

**Note:** For the purposes of your case analysis, calculating an annual ROI and payback is sufficient.

- However, it is important to note that for multi-year investment decisions, the time value of money should be taken into account.

- The net present value (NPV) of an investment decision can be calculated using a discount rate and future annual cash flows. This topic will be covered in detail in more advanced financial analysis courses.

## Differential Framework

1. Outline **Alternatives**
2. Evaluate the **qualitative factors** involved in the decision.
3. Identify the **relevant items** (ie. those that are future, cash and different).
4. **Separate:** 

![[Pasted image 20250225093533.png]]

5. Calculate **ROI/PAYBACK**
6. **EVALUATE ROI against hurdle rate** (if available)
	- If hurdle rate not given, evaluate the risk of the venture versus the return and make the call.
7. Perform **sensitivity analysis** (if necessary)
	- Consider variables that are subject to significant uncertainty and that are large enough to potentially alter conclusions.

8. Make a **DECISION**
