## Condensed Income Statements

Income statements can be presented in more than one way depending on how costs are classifies. The Term 1 statement classifies costs as **products** or **period**; whereas, the income statement, on a contribution basis, classifies costs as **fixed** or **variable**

### Uses of Contribution Analysis

There are a number of uses of contribution analysis

***Financial Projections/Budgeting***

steps for easier projected income statements

a. Determine selling price per unit  
b. Forecast the number of units to be sold  
c. Classify all costs as fixed or variable  
d. Calculate variable cost as a % of sales

Now, the costs of the firm have been broken down into four variables: 
	-> selling price, units to be sold, variable costs and fixed costs.

**Sub-Unit Evaluation**

Developing statements on a sub-unit basis can point to problems that might go undetected using the overall net income figures

	Example.
		 Acme Company’s last income statement read as follows

![[Pasted image 20250107050719.png]]

Breaking down the contribution on a product line basis, Acme determines that one of the products has a negative contribution.

![[Pasted image 20250107050757.png]]

**Product A** and **Product C** help contribute towards fixed costs share by the business as a whole. **Product B** does not.

The $100,000 profit level hides the unprofitable **Product B**. The contribution analysis highlights that management must either:

- Improve **Product B** or
- Eliminate **Product B**, if sales to **Products A** and **C** would not be affected by its loss

**Pricing** 

Compare total contribution between two price points and two volume points to determine the financial implications of a price change.

	Example 
		Assume that Sox R Us is currently selling 100,000 pairs of socks @  
		$2/pair. Variable cost is $0.90/pair. Total fixed costs are $100,000. 
		Sox R Us figures that it could sell 150,000 pairs @ $1.50/pair. The  
		fixed costs and variable cost per unit would remain the same at both  
		volumes

	Should Sox R Us Lower prices?

	Answer: No, Sox R Us should not lower prices. While sales revenue increases from $200,000 (100,000 units @ $2 each), to $225,000 (150,000 units @ $1.50 each), total contribution is lower under the low price scenario

![[Pasted image 20250107051446.png]]

Since fixed costs are the same in each scenario, total contribution figures are sufficient to make a decision.
