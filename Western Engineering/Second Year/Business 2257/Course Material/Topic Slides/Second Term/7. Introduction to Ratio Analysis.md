 
**USE:**
- **External:** Investors, bankers, and analysts
- **INTERNAL:** Key step in financial size-up Aid in operations and financing decisions

**WHY RATIOS?**
- Compare on a base case (eg a %) vs. absolute number

## Measurement Ideas

1. **Profitability**
- How Profitable is the venture? 
	- Example: return from sales; return from capital

2. **Investment Utilization**
- Measures the balance between sales/profit and asset (particularly fixed assets and inventory)
- Sometimes known as efficiency ratios.

3. **Liquidity**
- Measures the ability of the company to meet short-term debts.
- Also concerned with the efficiency of working capital investments - receivables, inventory and payables. 

## Measurement Areas

4. **Stability**
- Measures the balance between debt and equity.
- Debt, in manageable amounts is good. 
- Debt financing allows ownership to be retained and is cheaper than equity (ie. debt payments -- interest -- are expenses and therefore tax deductible; equity payments -- dividends -- are not deductible).
- However, too much debt increases the risk of insolvency.

5. **Growth**
- Measures improvement (or decline in performance from year to year)

# Limitations of Ratios

1. Quantitative only.
2. Standards of comparison imperfect.
3. Difficult to compare with other firms.
4. Different definitions for same ratio.
5. Inflation may lead to misleading results.
6. Bias during rapid growth stage or industry decline.
7. Prepared annually -- lots of change in between.
8. Tell us what has happened, **not** what will happen. 

**However, despite these limitations, proper use of financial ratios, when mixed with some common sense. can provide invaluable insight for managers and investors alike.**

**Note:**

A ratio can be high or low for two reasons:
- A ratio is **HIGH** because:
	The numerator is high OR The denominator is low

- A ratio is **LOW** because:
	The numerator is low **OR** The denominator is high.

**When interpreting the ratio, try to ascertain which part of the fraction is the primary cause of the number being high or low**

**Subject To:**
- Qualitative factors
- Industry trends
- External factors
- Ratio definitions
- Differing accounting policies
- Changes in accounting policies
- Time frame of analysis
- Suitability of historical data as a prediction tool

## Conceptual Framework (for cases involving ratio analysis)

1. **DO A SIZE-UP OF THE FIRM**
	Consider: 
		- Past performance
		- Industry trends
		- Level or urgency/merit

2. **IDENTIFY IMPORTANT RATIOS**
	Consider:
		- Results from statement of cash flows
		- Your role in the case

3. **DO THE NUMBERS**
	Avoid: 
		- Redundancy
		- Meaningless numbers

4. **DRAW CONCLUSIONS**
	Consider:
		- The "FIT" between your qualitative and quantitative analysis
		- Corrective action
		- The reasons behind a misfit
