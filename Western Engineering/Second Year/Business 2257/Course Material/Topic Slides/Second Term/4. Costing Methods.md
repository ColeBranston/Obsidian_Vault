**Reminder:** Factory Overhead: The indirect costs associated with the manufacturing process.
Ex. -> Indirect Labor (Supervisors), Utilities, Depreciation, Rent

>[!Cost/Price Relationships]
>**Business Goal:** make money
>Therefore, **PRICE** charged must **EXCEE** **COST**
>- Example: Don't charge $5 when it costs $7

>[!important] Definition of Cost
>**Problem**: several definitions of cost.
>- Direct Cost
>- Absorption Cost
>- Full Cost
>
>Which definition of cost should be used when setting prices?

>[!important] Direct Cost
>**Direct Cost Per Unit:**
>- Include only the **Direct, Traceable** costs.
>	- Direct material
>	- Direct labour
>	- Direct selling expenses
>
>- For all intents and purposes, **direct cost/unit = variable cost/unit**.
>- Direct cost per unit does NOT change with changes in volume.
>
>
>**Uses of Direct Cost Per Unit:**
>- Setting a floor price
>- Contribution analysis
>- Breakeven calculations

>[!important] Absorption Cost
>- Include **All Production Costs** regardless of their classification as fixed or variable.
>	- Direct Materials.
>	- Direct Labor
>	- Factory Overhead
>
>- Can be calculated using the financial statements of manufacturing companies: 
>$${\text{Cost of Finished Goods Manufactured}\over \text{Units Manufactured}} \text{ or } {\text{Cost Of Goods Sold}\over Units Sold}$$
>- Absorption cost per unit changes depending on volume.
>
>
>**Uses of Absorption Cost Per Unit:**
>- Comparing relative plant efficiency
>- Financial Reporting

>[!important] Full Cost
>**Full Cost Per Unit:**
>- Includes **All Costs** in the price per unit
>	- Direct Materials
>	- Direct Labor
>	- All factory overhead
>	- All selling expenses
>	- All general and administrative charges
>	- All financing
>- May need to make use of allocation rates to calculate full cost on a per unit basis
>- Full cost per unit changes depending on volume.
>
>
>**Uses of Full Cost Per Unit:**
>- Long-term pricing strategy/determining normal price
>- Defense and cost-plus contracts

### Cost/Price Relationships

- Choose the cost definition that best suits the problem at-hand.
- Also, remember:
	- **Intelligent Pricing Decisions** can only be made after a study of both **internal costs** and **external demand**.

**The following factors should be considered when making pricing decisions:**

$\checkmark$ Consumer's willingness to pay
$\checkmark$ Competitive Prices
$\checkmark$ Industry Conditions
