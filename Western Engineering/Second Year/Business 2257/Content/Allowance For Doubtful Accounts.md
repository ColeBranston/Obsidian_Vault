First, if we know we are not going to be able to collect the entire value  
of our accounts receivable, we need a way of signaling to the users of  
our statements the dollar value of credit accounts we expect we will  
be unable to collect.

By using a **contra asset** account called **Allowance for Doubtful**  
**Accounts** we are able to lower the value of our accounts receivable  
to represent what we realistically expect to be able to collect from our  
credit customers

**Allowance for Doubtful Accounts (A4DA)** has a normal **credit**  
**balance** as it is a contra asset. This allows the account to lower the  
value of accounts receivable. It is an **estimation** of the accounts that  
will not be collectable during the period.

![[Pasted image 20240921215926.png]]

Combined with the accounts receivable balance, **A4DA** shows the net realizable value of accounts receivable as they would appear on the  
balance sheet.

![[Pasted image 20240921220029.png]]

The initial estimation in **A4DA** seldom coincides with the exact value  
of accounts that are uncollectable, or “go bad” during the period. To  
account for this discrepancy we must keep record of all the accounts  
that go bad during the period by performing a **write-off transaction.**

A write-off will lower the balance in **A4DA** as a way of using a portion  
of the allowance we had for uncollectable accounts.
