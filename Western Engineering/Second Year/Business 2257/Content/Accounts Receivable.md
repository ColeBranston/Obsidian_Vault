- Represents money owed to a company **by its customers** for goods and services purchased with credit

- Sale is treated as an accounts receivable after a customer is billed an **invoice** for the goods and/or services received

- Considered a **trade account**

- Can be separated by customer (subsidiary ledger) but will always be combined into one account (general ledger) in Business 2257.

We assume when we make a credit sale that the customer will pay;  
however, that is not always the case:

- Sometimes the customer pays **late**
- Sometimes the customer **never** pays

Thus there is a valid business cost associated with offering trade  
credit: an expense from uncollectible accounts. One would expect  
that as credit sales increase, this expense would also increase.
