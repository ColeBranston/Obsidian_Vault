Sometimes a company will write-off an account expecting not to receive payment, and then will unexpectedly receive payment for the previously written-off account. When this situation occurs, we must **recover** the previously written-off account and re-instate the account as an accounts receivable.

We perform a recovery of the account as it is now collectable and  
should be included as an accounts receivable.

**Recovery**: Re-instating and collecting an accounts receivable that  
had been previously written off as worthless.

**Accounting Procedure:** Assume that a business wrote off an  
account from a customer who owed $200. Unexpectedly, the  
money was paid. First, the business must reinstate the customers  
account by reversing the write-off process. Next, the collection can  
be recorded like any other cash collection.

## End of Period Adjusting Entry

At the end of each period, one adjusting entry is made to **Bad Debt**  
**Expense (DR)** and **Allowance for Doubtful Accounts (CR)**

The bad debt expense represents:  
- Accounts that have gone bad from this period’s sales  
- Accounts that might go bad from this period’s sales.

## The Accounting Problem

- Accounts are seldom written off in the same period in which the sale was made (expense will not match revenue)  
- Must **estimate** the number or amount of accounts that will be written off in subsequent periods, and record the **expense** in **this** period.
