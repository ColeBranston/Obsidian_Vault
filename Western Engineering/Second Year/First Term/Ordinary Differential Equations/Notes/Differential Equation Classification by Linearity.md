An *n*th order ordinary differential equation is said to be linear in the variable y if F is linear in y, y, ..., y<sup>(n)</sup>

This essentially leaves us with a couple of rules:
- **The dependent variable and all of its derivatives must be of the first degree** (e.g., if $y$ were the dependent variable and $y^2$ was in the equation, it is nonlinear)
- **The coefficient of one of the derivatives of the dependent variable must not include the dependent variable** (e.g. if $y$ were the dependent variable, we cannot have $(y-x)\frac{dy}{dx}$ be one of the terms as y would be in the coefficient of $\frac{dy}{dx}$)
- **The function of the term must be linear** (e.g. if $y$ was our dependent variable, then $siny$ cannot be a term in the [[Differential Equation]])
