Work done by a constant force F in moving an object over a distance *s* :

$$W = Fscos \theta$$
$$= F*S$$![[Pasted image 20240118121016.png]]

If the force is the electric force of a uniform field E, then the work done by the electric force to move for a charge q over a distance *s* is:

$$W = Fscos \theta = qEscos \theta $$
$$ = F * S = qE*S$$![[Pasted image 20240118121143.png]]

- If the force varies with position along the path s, the work done by the force in moving an object from A to B is given by:
$$W_{AB} = \int ^B _A F*ds$$

- For a charge q0 in a non-uniform field E the work done by the electric force $F = q_0 E$ is: 
$$W_{AB} = q_0 \int _A ^B E*ds$$
- where the integral is along the path followed from A to B.

![[Pasted image 20240118121348.png]]

**For a conservative force (e.g. gravity, the electric force) the work done by the force in moving and object from point A to point B is independent of the path taken by the particle.**

- We can write the work done by the electric field as the difference in the electric potential energy

$$W_{AB} = \int ^B _A F * ds$$
$$=-(U_B - U_A)$$
→ Here $U_a \text{ and } U_B$ are the potential energy at points A and B respectively.

![[Pasted image 20240118121731.png]]

**Note: The work done by the field is just the negative of the difference in potential energy between the final point B and the initial point A**

	- How much work is done by the electric field of charge q (located at the origin) in moving charge q0 from point A to point B? We can choose any path from A to B that we like. Since the electric field due to q is radial, the math is simpler if we choose a path that is also radial, so 𝑠⃗ is parallel to 𝐸.

The work done by the electric field of charge *q* is then:

$$W = q_0 \int _A ^B E *ds$$
$$= q_0 \int ^B _A Eds$$
$$=q_0 \int _A ^B \frac{kq}{r^2}dr$$
$$=kqq_0 \int _A ^B \frac{dr}{r^2}$$
$$=-kqq_0[\frac{1}{r}]_A ^B = -(\frac{kqq_0}{r_B} - \frac{kqq_0}{r_A})$$
