- The electric potential of a point charge is: $V= \frac{kq}{r}$
- The electric potential of a distribution of charges can be calculated using the superposition principle: 
	- we add up the potentials due to each charge in the distribution

$$V_P = V_1 +V_2 +V_3$$
$$=\frac{kq_1}{r_1}+\frac{kq_2}{r_2}+\frac{kq_3}{r_3}$$

- **Because V is a scalar, it is generally easier to calculate the electric potential due a collection of point charges than to calculate the electric field!**

- The potential at a point P due to a single point charge Q is: $V= k\frac{Q}{r}$
- The potential of a collection of *N* point charges is: 
$$V=k \sum _{i=1} ^{N} \frac{Q_i}{r_i}$$

# Potential of a continuous charge distribution

- To find the potential of a continuous distribution of charge, we have to integrate over all the contributions to the potential.

$$V_P = \int dV = \int \frac{dq}{r}$$
