- So far we have assumed that the gap between the plates of a capacitor is filled with air. 
- Real capacitors usually have a thin layer of insulating material – a dielectric – sandwiched between the plates. 
	- It’s purpose is partially to keep the conductors separated 
	- As we shall see, dielectrics can also increase the energy storage capacity. 
- Dielectrics contain electric dipoles, but (almost) no free charges.
<br>
- Dipoles orient in the field of the capacitor in such a way that the field inside the capacitor is decreased.

- Using a dielectric increases the capacitance C.

- Dielectrics are characterized by a dielectric constant κ. 
- Electric field in the capacitor decreases to:
$$E = \frac{E_0}{k} = \frac{\sigma}{k\epsilon_0}$$
- Potential decreases to:
$$V = \frac{V_0}{k} = \frac{E_0d}{k}$$
- Capacitance of a parallel-plate capacitor filled with dielectric increases to:

$$C = kC_0 =\frac{k\epsilon_0A}{d}$$
- Stored energy decreases to:
$$U = \frac{1}{2}CV^2 = \frac{1}{2} k C_0(\frac{V_0}{k})^2=\frac{U_0}{k}$$

**Application of Capacitors and Dielectrics: Electric Stud Finders**

- A metal plate in the stud finder acts as one half of a capacitor. The wall is the other half. 
- Wires or studs inside the wall change the effective dielectric constant.
- This changes the capacitance and triggers a signal (useful work).

# Application of Capacitors and Dielectrics: Information Transmission

- A coaxial cable consists of a central wire (usually copper), surrounded by a layer of insulator, then another conducting shield, and an outer protective jacket. 
- The central core transmits information. 
- Because the E field inside of a conductor is 0, the conducting shield protects the core from electromagnetic interference. 
- The dielectric insulator separates the core from the conducting shield. 
- Common applications include video and CATV distribution, RF and microwave transmission, and computer and instrumentation data connections.
