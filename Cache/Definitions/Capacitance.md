$$V=\frac{Qd}{\epsilon_0 A}$$

Rearranging... we get: $Q = (\frac{A\epsilon_0}{d})V = CV$ 

- *C* is called the “capacitance” of our parallel plate capacitor.

$$C = \frac{Q}{V} = \frac{A\epsilon_0}{d}$$
- ***C* depends only on the geometry of the plates, and on the material in between them.**

- C is the charge stored on the capacitor plates per volt of potential difference across the plates.
- Units = [C/V] = **farads** [F] 
- Typical capacitors have capacitance in the pF (picofarad) – mF (milifarad) range.
