- In electrostatic equilibrium: all charges stationary; nothing moves; nothing changes over time. *Electrostatics*. 
- What happens if there is a net force, and the charges are allowed to move?
- → if charges move, we have an electric current – the rate at which charges pass by. *Electrodynamics*.

![[Pasted image 20240129104841.png]]
- We define the current at a point as:

$$I = \frac{\Delta Q}{\Delta t}$$
- Where ΔQ is the amount of charge passing our point in a time interval Δt.

- For variable current, or small time intervals:

$$I = \lim_{\Delta t → 0}\frac{\Delta Q}{\Delta t} = \frac{dQ}{dt}$$

- Note that dQ/dt is not really a derivative – it is just the amount of charge passing a point per unit time.
- Usually written as scalar quantity with a sign indicating which way the current is flowing.
- Convention: current flows in direction of flow of positive charge. 
- Thus: current flows in opposite direction as flow of electrons! 
- Units: C/s = Ampère (Amps); denote by A.
- Real world currents: from pA to kA.
