
**Leadership Principles**

>[!important] Customer Obsession
>Leaders start with the customer and work backwards. They work vigorously to earn and keep customer trust. Although leaders pay attention to competitors, they obsess over customers. 

>[!important] Ownership
>Leaders are owners. They think long term and don't sacrifice long-term value for short-term results. They act on behalf of the entire company, beyond just their own team. They never say "that's not my job".

>[!important] Invent and Simplify
>Leaders expect and require innovation and invention from their teams and always find ways to simplify. They are externally aware, look for new ideas from everywhere, and are not limited by "not invented here". As we do new things, we accept that we may be misunderstood for long periods of time. 

>[!important] Are Right, A Lot
>Leaders are right a lot. They have strong judgement and good instincts. They seek diverse perspectives and work to disconfirm their beleifs.

>[!important] Learn and Be Curious
>Leaders are never done learning and always seek to improve themselves. They are curious about new possibilities and act to explore them.

>[!important] Hire and Develop the Best
>Leaders raise the performance bar with every hire and promotion. They recognize exceptional talent, and willingly move them throughout the organization. Leaders develop leaders and take seriously their role in coaching others. We work on behalf of our people to invent mechanisms for development like Career Choice. 

>[!important] Insist on the Highest Standards
>Leaders have relentlessly high standards -- many people may think these standards are unreasonably high. Leaders are continually raising the bar and drive their teams to deliver high-quality products, services, and processes.  Leaders ensure that defects do not get sent down the line and that problem are fixed so they stay fixed.

>[!important] Think Big
>Thinking small is a self-fulfilling  prophecy. Leaders create and communicate  bold direction that inspires results. They think differently and look around corners for ways to server customers. 

>[!important] Bias For Action
>Speed matters in business. Many decisions and actions are reversible and do not need extensive study. We value calculated risk taking.

>[!important] Frugality
>Accomplish more with less. Constraints breed resourcefulness, self-sufficiency and invention. There are no extra points for growing headcount, budget size, or fixed expense. 

>[!important] Earn Trust
>Leaders listen attentively, speak candidly, and treat others respectfully. They are vocally self-critical, even when doing  so it award or embarrassing. Leaders do not believe their or their team's body odor smells of perfume. They benchmark themselves and their teams against best. 

>[!important] Dive Deep
>Leaders operate at all levels, stay connected to the details, audit frequently, and are skeptical when metrics and anecdote differ. No task is beneath them.

>[!important] Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit
>Leaders are obligated to respectfully challenge decisions when they disagree, even when doing so is uncomfortable or exhausting. Leaders have conviction and are tenacious. they do not compromise for the sake of social cohesion. Once a decision is determined, they commit wholly. 

>[!important] Deliver Results
>Leaders focus on the key inputs for their business and deliver them with the right quality and in a timely fashion. Despite setbacks, they rise to the occasion and never settle. 

>[!important] Strive to be Earth's Best Employer
>Leaders work every day to create a safer, more productive, higher performing, more diverse, and more just work environment. They lead with empathy, have fun at work, and make it easy for others to have fun. Leaders ask themselves: are my fellow employees growing? Are they empowered? Are they ready for what's next? Leaders have a vision for and commitment to their employees' personal success, whether that be at Amazon or elsewhere.

>[!important] Success and Scale Bring Broad Responsibility
>We started in a garage, but we're not there anymore. We are big, we impact the world, and we are far from perfect. We must be humble and thoughtful about even the secondary effects of our actions. Our local communities, planet, and future generations need us to be better every day. We must begin each day with a determination to make better, do better, and be better for out customers, our employees, our partners, and the world at large.  And we must end every day knowing we can do even more tomorrow. Leaders create more than they consumer and always leave things better than how they found them.

## **1. Updating Proxy and Deployment Configs for Spring Boot APIs**

- **Leadership Principles:** Ownership, Dive Deep, Deliver Results
- **Best for Answering Questions Like:**
    - _Tell me about a time you took ownership of a project beyond your direct responsibilities._
    - _Describe a time you had to dive deep to solve a complex problem._
    - _Have you ever worked under strict constraints or compliance requirements? How did you handle it?_

### **STAR Response**

**Situation:**  
At my internship at Sun Life Financial, I was responsible for updating **proxy and deployment configurations** for several Spring Boot APIs. The task involved strict compliance and security guidelines, and a misconfiguration could have led to service downtime.

**Task:**  
I needed to update the configurations to meet **new security policies** while ensuring a smooth deployment with minimal disruption. However, there was **limited documentation**, and understanding all dependencies required thorough investigation.

**Action:**  
I took full **ownership** by thoroughly auditing the existing configurations and **diving deep** into system logs to understand the root structure of our deployment pipeline. Since documentation was outdated, I worked with senior developers to validate my approach and tested changes in a **sandbox environment** before pushing to production.

**Result:**  
The deployment was successful with **zero downtime** and full compliance. I also created **detailed documentation**, which became a reference for future updates. This project reinforced the importance of **taking ownership beyond my immediate responsibilities and ensuring long-term solutions rather than quick fixes**.

## **2. Teaching Python to Grade 7 Students**

- **Leadership Principles:** Earn Trust, Hire and Develop the Best, Think Big
- **Best for Answering Questions Like:**
    - _Tell me about a time you mentored someone or helped others grow._
    - _Have you ever led an initiative that required you to earn trust from others?_
    - _Describe a situation where you thought big and inspired others to take action._

### **STAR Response**

**Situation:**  
I noticed a lack of accessible programming education in my community, so I **volunteered to teach Python** to **100 Grade 7 students** at a local elementary school. Many students had no prior coding experience, and keeping them engaged was a challenge.

**Task:**  
My goal was to make programming **fun and interactive** while ensuring that students grasped fundamental coding concepts. I had to adapt my teaching methods to accommodate different learning speeds and interests.

**Action:**  
I designed a curriculum focused on **hands-on learning**, incorporating small coding challenges and interactive games to make the material engaging. To **earn trust**, I actively encouraged questions, celebrated progress, and provided one-on-one support when needed.

**Result:**  
By the end of the sessions, students were able to **build basic games and automation scripts** in Python. Some even **expressed interest in pursuing programming further**. This experience demonstrated the value of **thinking big**, **developing others**, and **earning trust** by meeting students where they were in their learning journey.

## **3. Hackathon: Debate Bot with Cosine & RAG Techniques**

- **Leadership Principles:** Invent and Simplify, Bias for Action, Are Right, A Lot
- **Best for Answering Questions Like:**
    - _Tell me about a time you had to quickly adapt to meet a deadline._
    - _Describe a situation where you had to innovate or simplify a process._
    - _Have you ever made a judgment call under pressure? What was the outcome?_

### **STAR Response**

**Situation:**  
During a **hackathon**, my team aimed to build a **debate bot** that could **generate real-time arguments** using **cosine similarity and Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) techniques**. We had only **one week**, but underestimated the time required to fine-tune our NLP model.

**Task:**  
As the deadline approached, we realized we wouldn’t be able to submit on time unless we made **quick, strategic decisions** on which features were essential and which could be dropped.

**Action:**  
Instead of panicking, I **prioritized the core functionalities**, focusing on **optimizing the retrieval pipeline** rather than perfecting the argument generation. I also reached out to the hackathon organizers and found out there was a **grace period** available, which gave us a little extra time to finalize our submission.

**Result:**  
Our project was **successfully submitted**, and we received **positive feedback on its technical depth**. While we didn’t win, our ability to **simplify under pressure** and **take calculated risks** allowed us to **deliver a working product** despite the setbacks. This experience reinforced the importance of **making quick, informed decisions and adapting rapidly in high-stakes situations**.
